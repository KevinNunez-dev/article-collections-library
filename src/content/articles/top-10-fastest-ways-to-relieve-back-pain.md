## The short answer

**The fastest relief usually comes from stacking a few simple steps: stay gently active, calm the muscles that are guarding, and stop repeating what set the pain off.** If it keeps coming back or won't settle, targeted care aimed at the muscles involved can shorten the road.

Below are ten approaches, roughly ordered from things you can do today to care that goes further. Not every item fits every person, and none of them replaces an assessment when symptoms are serious or lasting.

## The top 10

### 1. Keep moving with low-impact activity

Rest for a short time if you need to, then get up. Easy walking, swimming, or gentle yoga is usually kinder to a sore back than bed rest, and regular low-impact activity helps the muscles that support the spine. A common goal is about 30 minutes, a few days a week, once the flare-up calms. Stop if pain turns sharp or spreads.

### 2. Fix posture and change position often

Long stretches of sitting, standing, or driving can keep a sore back locked up. Sit with your feet flat and your back supported, stand with weight spread evenly on both feet, and switch positions every 20 to 30 minutes. For desk-related pain, see [how to relieve back pain from sitting](/health/how-to-relieve-back-pain-from-sitting/) and our page on [poor posture](/health/poor-posture/).

### 3. Use heat for tight, stiff muscles

A warm shower, heating pad, or warm pack for up to 20 minutes can loosen tight muscles and make movement easier. Keep a layer between the heat and your skin.

### 4. Try ice for a fresh flare-up

If the pain started suddenly after an awkward lift or twist, a cold pack wrapped in a cloth may dull the sharpness and help with swelling. Keep sessions to about 20 minutes, and choose whichever of heat or ice feels better to you. Skip both if you are using a topical cream.

### 5. Try a position of relief

Lying on your back with your calves resting on a chair, or on your side with a pillow between your knees, can take load off the low back. Another option is lying on the floor with a rolled towel under your pelvis and letting your hips relax. Use these for a few minutes to calm a spasm before you try to move.

### 6. Do gentle, pain-free stretches

Easy stretches can reduce the pull on the low back. Good starting points are child's pose, cat-cow, a single knee to the chest, and a light hamstring or glute stretch. Hold gently and breathe, and never stretch into sharp pain. These muscles often matter as much as the back itself, which is why [muscle stiffness](/health/muscle-stiffness/) in the hips and legs can show up as back pain.

### 7. Build core and hip support once the flare-up eases

A stronger core and steadier hips share the load that would otherwise fall on the lower back. Start with simple exercises such as bridges and bird-dogs, or a beginner yoga, Pilates, or tai chi class, and progress gradually. Also reduce the specific lifting, bending, or workout that provokes symptoms rather than stopping everything. Our guide on [5 ways to reduce back pain](/health/top-5-ways-to-reduce-back-pain/) covers workload and workstation changes.

### 8. Calm the body with breathing and relaxation

Pain and stress feed each other, and tense muscles can stay braced long after the original strain. Slow deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation (tensing and releasing one muscle group at a time), or a few minutes of mindfulness can help the body let go. Research on these approaches is still developing, but they are low-risk and easy to try.

### 9. Consider over-the-counter and topical options

Some people use over-the-counter pain relievers or rub-on creams for short-term comfort. Check with a pharmacist or doctor first, especially if you have other conditions or take other medications. They ease symptoms but do not address why the back is guarding. Over the longer term, sleep position (a pillow between the knees when on your side), supportive footwear, a healthy weight, and not smoking all help take stress off the spine.

### 10. Get targeted care for the muscles driving the pain

When pain lingers or keeps returning, muscle tension and movement restrictions around the spine, hips, and glutes are common contributors. Care that finds and addresses those areas can often work faster than waiting. This is where Robotic Precision Therapy fits.

## When to get urgent or prompt care

Seek urgent medical care if back pain comes with:

- Progressive weakness in a leg or foot
- Numbness in the groin or saddle area
- New bowel or bladder changes
- Fever, unexplained weight loss, or severe pain at night with no relief
- Pain after a fall, accident, or other significant trauma

See a clinician soon if pain is severe, travels down a leg, or has not improved after a couple of weeks. Our guide on [when to see a doctor for back pain](/health/when-to-see-a-doctor-for-back-pain/) explains more.
