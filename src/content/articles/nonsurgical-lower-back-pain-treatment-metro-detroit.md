## A non-surgical starting point around Metro Detroit

For many people with lower back pain, a reasonable non-surgical starting point is a safety check followed by a conversation about activity, goals, and conservative options. That may involve a primary care clinician, a physical therapist, or both. It does not mean every person needs the same treatment—or that an appointment count can be chosen from a website.

This overview is for people in Detroit, Troy, Royal Oak, Rochester, Warren, and nearby communities. It cannot diagnose the cause of pain or replace individual medical advice.

## 1. Start with safety and the right level of care

Seek urgent evaluation for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the saddle area, rapidly worsening weakness, or severe pain after significant trauma. Fever or other signs of serious illness should also be discussed promptly with a medical professional. Do not use a calculator or online article to decide that warning signs are safe to ignore.

When pain is not an emergency, a primary care clinician can consider medical causes and medication questions. A physical therapist can assess movement and function and discuss an exercise-based plan. Which door is most practical can depend on your health history, access, and insurance rules.

## 2. Understand what “conservative care” can include

Conservative care is a broad term. It can include education, modifying activity for a time, staying as active as tolerated, exercise, and physical therapy. Some people discuss medication or other adjuncts with their prescriber. The American College of Physicians guideline for nonradicular low back pain describes non-drug approaches for different symptom durations; the 2021 JOSPT guideline reviews physical therapy interventions. These sources do not guarantee an outcome or establish that every modality works equally well for every person.

Read the [ACP guideline summary](https://www.acponline.org/acp-newsroom/american-college-of-physicians-issues-guideline-for-treating-nonradicular-low-back-pain) and [JOSPT clinical practice guideline](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2021.0508). Ask a provider to explain which recommendation applies to your situation and what the evidence does not answer.

## 3. Compare care providers by process

For any Metro Detroit clinic, ask who evaluates you, how goals will be agreed, how progress is measured, what happens if symptoms worsen, and when the provider refers to a physician or specialist. A thoughtful plan should be responsive to the person—not a guarantee based on a particular brand or a fixed series of visits.
