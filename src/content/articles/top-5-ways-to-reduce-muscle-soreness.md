## 1. Keep Movement Gentle

Comfortable walking, easy cycling, or gentle range-of-motion work may feel better than complete inactivity. Avoid turning soreness into sharp pain by forcing intensity too soon.

## 2. Progress Training Gradually

New exercises, more volume, hills, or heavier loads can increase soreness. Build changes over time and include recovery days after unfamiliar sessions.

## 3. Support Basic Recovery

Sleep, hydration, regular meals, and adequate recovery time support your ability to adapt to exercise. There is no single shortcut that replaces them.

## 4. Pay Attention to Pain Quality

Diffuse soreness that improves gradually is different from a sudden focal pain, a pop, bruising, or weakness. These symptoms may need evaluation.
