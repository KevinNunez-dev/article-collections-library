## What Is Groin Pain?

The groin is where the inner thigh meets the lower abdomen and pelvis. **Pain in the groin** may be sharp, aching, pulling, or burning, and it may appear on one side or both. It can follow sprinting, kicking, lifting, or a sudden change of direction, or begin gradually with no clear event.

Because many structures sit in this small area, a careful assessment is more reliable than guessing from location alone.

## Common Causes of Groin Pain by Structure

### Hip Joint and Adductor (Inner Thigh) Muscles

The adductor muscles on the inner thigh control leg movement and are frequently strained in running, hockey, soccer, and other sports. The hip joint itself refers pain to the groin, particularly with deep flexion or rotation. See [hip pain](/health/hip-pain) and [muscle strain](/health/muscle-strain).

### Abdominal Wall

The lower abdominal and groin area can be stressed by lifting, twisting, and core-demanding sport. Pain with coughing or sneezing, or a new bulge, should be evaluated by a medical provider.

### Pelvic Contributors

Pelvic joint and pelvic floor muscle tension can contribute to groin symptoms in some people. Pelvic pain with fever, unusual discharge, bleeding, or urinary symptoms needs medical evaluation.

### Hernia-Related Causes

A hernia can cause groin pain or a visible bulge that is more obvious with standing or straining. This requires medical examination; it is not something to self-diagnose.

### Nerve-Related Causes

Irritation of nerves in the pelvis, hip, or low back can cause burning, tingling, or shooting groin pain. See [sciatica](/health/sciatica).

### Lower Back Referred Pain

Problems in the low back or sacroiliac region can refer pain to the groin and front of the thigh. See [lower back pain](/health/lower-back-pain) and [sacroiliac joint pain](/health/sacroiliac-joint-pain).

## Groin Pain by Movement Context

| Context | What it may suggest |
| --- | --- |
| Walking | Hip joint or adductor irritation, pelvic or back contributors |
| Running | Adductor overuse, hip mobility limits, core control |
| Squatting | Hip joint or deep-flexion irritation, adductor strain |
| Getting out of a car | Hip joint irritation, adductor sensitivity |
| Coughing or sneezing | Abdominal wall tension; hernia should be ruled out |
| Kicking or cutting | Adductor strain, hip flexor involvement |

## Right-Sided Groin Pain in Women

Right-sided groin pain in women can be musculoskeletal, such as an adductor strain, hip contributor, or abdominal wall tension. It can also relate to pelvic, gynecologic, urinary, or digestive causes that require medical assessment. Seek medical care promptly for right groin or lower abdominal pain with fever, vomiting, pregnancy, unusual bleeding, or sudden severe pain. For a deeper look, see [right-side groin pain in women](/health/right-side-groin-pain-in-women).

For men, see [groin pain in men](/health/groin-pain-in-men).

## How Physical Therapy Evaluates Groin Pain

A physical therapy assessment separates the contributors that matter for your recovery:

- **Mobility:** hip rotation, flexion, and back motion
- **Strength:** adductors, hip flexors, glutes, and core
- **Gait:** walking and running mechanics
- **Sport loading:** training volume, cutting, kicking, and sprint demands
- **Safety screening:** symptoms that need referral to a physician

If findings suggest a musculoskeletal pattern, RPT Clinic can use gentle, targeted Robotic Precision Therapy pressure to selected tissues around the hip and thigh, combined with mobility, strengthening, and a gradual return-to-activity plan.

## Self-Care While You Arrange an Assessment

- Reduce sprinting, kicking, and wide-stance movements that provoke pain
- Keep walking in a pain-comfortable range
- Avoid aggressive stretching of a sore inner thigh
- Use warmth or cold, whichever feels better
- Return gradually once movement is comfortable
