## Understanding Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

Carpal tunnel syndrome occurs when the median nerve becomes compressed as it passes through the carpal tunnel—a narrow passage in your wrist about the size of your thumb.

Nine tendons and the median nerve pass through this tight space. The median nerve controls:
- **Sensation** in the thumb, index, middle, and part of the ring finger
- **Muscle function** in the thumb and fingers
- **Hand dexterity** and fine motor control

When the tunnel becomes crowded, the nerve gets compressed, creating:
- **Tingling or numbness** in thumb and fingers
- **Burning discomfort** in the palm and hand
- **Weakness** in grip strength or hand control
- **Symptoms at night** (often waking you from sleep)
- **Pain radiating up the forearm**

## The Hidden Truth: Carpal Tunnel Isn't Just About Your Wrist

Most people think carpal tunnel is simply a structural problem with a narrow tunnel. **This is wrong.** Recent research identifies **muscle imbalance as the primary driver in 85% of carpal tunnel cases.**

### Why Your Wrist Alone Can't Cause Carpal Tunnel

The carpal tunnel is created by carpal bones and a ligament at the wrist. These structures rarely change size. **So if the tunnel isn't narrowing, why is the nerve getting compressed?**

**The answer: Muscles above and around the tunnel are pulling it tighter.**

### The Whole-Body Muscle Imbalance Pattern

When you spend hours at a computer, your entire upper body adapts to that position:
- **Your shoulders round forward**
- **Your neck juts out** (forward head posture)
- **Your forearm muscles become chronically tight**

This creates a domino effect that puts pressure on the median nerve all the way down at your wrist.

### The Three Key Muscle Imbalances Causing Carpal Tunnel

**1. Forward Head Posture & Neck Muscle Tightness**
Tight scalenes, upper traps, and sternocleidomastoid muscles create tension throughout the entire arm chain. This tension "pulls" down through your shoulder, arm, and eventually to your wrist.

**2. Shoulder Muscle Imbalance**
Tight pectoralis minor (from rounded shoulders) and weak back muscles create internal shoulder rotation. This compresses nerves and restricts blood flow throughout the entire arm.

**3. Forearm Muscle Tightness**
- **Tight pronators** (from constant computer positioning)
- **Tight flexor carpi radialis** (the primary wrist flexor)
- **Tight posterior wrist capsule** (pulling carpal bones forward into the tunnel)
- **Weak extensors** (allowing flexors to dominate)

This creates muscle imbalance that increases tunnel crowding and nerve compression.

## Common Carpal Tunnel Symptoms

- **Tingling or numbness** in thumb, index, middle fingers and part of ring finger
- **Burning discomfort** in the palm and hand
- **Weakness** in grip strength or hand control
- **Clumsiness** with fine motor tasks
- **Symptoms worse at night** (numbness waking you from sleep)
- **Pain radiating up the forearm** into shoulder and neck
- **Symptoms after prolonged hand use** (typing, gripping, driving)

## Why Traditional Treatments Fail

**Splints** restrict movement but don't address muscle imbalance. Symptoms return when you remove the splint.

**Injections** temporarily reduce inflammation but don't solve the crowding problem. Effects wear off and symptoms return.

**Surgery** cuts the ligament to create more space—but if muscles are still pulling the carpal bones into compression, the nerve remains compressed.

**None of these address the root cause: the muscle imbalances pulling the tunnel tighter.**
