## Who evaluates spinal stenosis near Detroit?

Spinal stenosis means narrowing in spaces around the spinal cord or nerves. A primary care clinician, rehabilitation physician, or spine specialist can evaluate the diagnosis and medical options. A physical therapist may be part of a plan to improve function when appropriate.

Some people with narrowing have no symptoms. For others, standing or walking brings on leg pain, numbness, or heaviness that eases with sitting or bending forward. Circulation and other conditions can produce similar complaints, so an assessment should not rely on the scan alone. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000441.htm) explains spinal stenosis and its symptoms.

## What should the evaluation cover?

Describe how far you can walk, how long you can stand, and what changes when you sit or lean forward. Mention balance problems, falls, numbness, weakness, and whether symptoms affect one or both legs. Bring an imaging report and a medication list if available.

A clinician may examine walking, strength, sensation, reflexes, and movement. They may also consider other causes of leg symptoms. Ask how the findings relate to any imaging, what goals are realistic, and whether a physician or spine specialist should be involved. See our [spinal stenosis guide](/health/spinal-stenosis).

## When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the saddle or groin area, or rapidly worsening leg weakness. New or worsening difficulty walking, balance changes, or weakness should also be discussed promptly with a physician.

## Questions about your options

- What findings suggest stenosis is contributing to my symptoms?
- Are other causes of walking-related leg symptoms being considered?
- Which activities are reasonable, and how should we track walking tolerance?
- What options are available if function does not improve?
- When would a surgical or other specialist opinion be appropriate?

Treatment may involve rehabilitation, medical management, or surgery in selected cases. Ask about potential benefits and limitations rather than assuming a scan determines the treatment.
