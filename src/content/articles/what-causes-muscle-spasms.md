## What Causes Muscle Spasms?

A muscle spasm is a sudden, involuntary contraction. **What causes muscle spasms** is rarely a single factor. More often, several small triggers stack up until a muscle reacts. For definitions, symptoms, relief, and red flags, start with our main guide to [muscle spasms](/health/muscle-spasms).

## The Main Triggers

### Overuse and Fatigue

Muscles that work beyond their conditioning, such as after a jump in exercise or a long shift, are more likely to cramp or spasm.

### Dehydration and Electrolyte Changes

Heat, heavy sweating, illness, and low fluid intake can raise the likelihood of cramping for some people. It is a useful factor to check but not a universal explanation.

### Stress

Stress raises muscle tension, particularly in the neck, shoulders, jaw, and low back, which can make those muscles more reactive.

### Repetitive Motion and Static Positions

Typing, tool use, driving, and standing keep the same muscles loaded. This is a frequent cause of back, neck, forearm, and hand spasms.

### Medication Considerations

Some medications are associated with muscle cramps. Review any recent changes with your prescriber.

### Nerve Irritation

An irritated spinal nerve can trigger spasm in the muscles it supplies. See [sciatic nerve pain in the leg](/health/sciatic-nerve-pain-in-leg).

### Prior Strain

After a pulled muscle, nearby muscles guard the area and may spasm later with activity. See [muscle strain](/health/muscle-strain).

## Why Do I Keep Getting Muscle Spasms?

When spasms recur in one place, look for the pattern behind them:

- **Mobility restrictions:** a stiff joint nearby pushes load into the muscle.
- **Strength deficits:** weak muscles force others to overwork. See [muscle weakness](/health/muscle-weakness).
- **Loading:** quick increases in work, training, or lifting.
- **Ergonomics:** workstation, driving, or sleep setups that keep a muscle in one position.
- **Chronic tension:** see [muscle tightness](/health/muscle-tightness) and [muscle stiffness](/health/muscle-stiffness).

## Body Spasms, Whole-Body Cramps, and Twitches

Muscle twitches and cramps are usually related to fatigue, caffeine, stress, and sleep. Whole-body cramps or spasms in many areas at once are less typical and should be discussed with a medical provider, especially with illness, a new medication, weakness, or confusion.
