## The short answer

At a first Robotic Precision Therapy appointment you can expect **a conversation, an assessment, and a decision**: whether treatment appears appropriate, and if so, what comes next. Nothing is meant to be rushed.

## Quick answers

<details style="border:1px solid #d7dee6;border-radius:8px;padding:.75rem 1rem;margin:.6rem 0">
<summary style="cursor:pointer;font-weight:600">What should I bring?</summary>

A list of symptoms and when they began, your medications, any imaging or prior treatment notes, and your insurance card if you plan to ask about coverage.

</details>

<details style="border:1px solid #d7dee6;border-radius:8px;padding:.75rem 1rem;margin:.6rem 0">
<summary style="cursor:pointer;font-weight:600">Is it painful?</summary>

Many people describe firm pressure over tight areas. Pressure can be adjusted, so say so if it is too much.

</details>

<details style="border:1px solid #d7dee6;border-radius:8px;padding:.75rem 1rem;margin:.6rem 0">
<summary style="cursor:pointer;font-weight:600">What if I am told RPT is not right for me?</summary>

That can happen. A good assessment includes saying when another type of care, or a physician, is the better first step.

</details>

## Before you arrive

Spend ten minutes preparing. It makes the visit more useful.

- **Write a timeline.** When did it start? Was there an injury?
- **Rate your pain.** Note what it feels like at its best and worst, from 0 to 10.
- **List triggers.** Sitting, walking, stairs, sleeping, lifting.
- **List what you have tried.** Medication, stretching, other therapy, and what happened.
- **Note your goal.** Walking without pain, returning to a sport, sleeping through the night.
- **Gather records.** Imaging reports, surgical history, and a medication list.

## What happens at the visit

### 1. Arrival and paperwork
You will complete forms covering your health history and symptoms. Confirm your appointment details with the clinic, as they can differ by location.

### 2. The conversation
The clinician asks about your symptoms, history, and goals. Expect questions about warning signs such as numbness, weakness, or changes in bladder and bowel control.

### 3. The assessment
You may be asked to stand, walk, bend, or move the painful area while the clinician observes. They may also feel for muscle tightness. Our guide to [how the evaluation works](/health/how-robotic-precision-therapy-evaluation-works) covers this in detail.

### 4. The decision
The clinician explains what they found, whether RPT appears appropriate, and what a plan might look like. This is the moment to ask about cost. See [cost, insurance, and common questions](/health/robotic-precision-therapy-cost-insurance-questions).

### 5. Treatment, if appropriate
The RX2600 Therapeutic Robot applies pressure to the areas the clinician selects. You can speak up at any time to change pressure or stop.

### 6. Wrap-up
You should leave knowing what was done, what to watch for, and what the next step is.

## After the appointment

- **Notice, do not judge too fast.** Track how you feel over the next day or two.
- **Hydrate and move gently** unless told otherwise.
- **Report anything unusual** to the clinic, such as increasing pain or new numbness.
- **Keep your notes.** They help the next visit.

## When not to wait for an appointment

Seek urgent medical care for severe pain after an injury, loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or saddle region, new leg or arm weakness, chest pain, or a swollen, red, painful leg.
