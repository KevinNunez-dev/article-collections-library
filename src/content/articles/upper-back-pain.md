## Understanding Upper Back Pain

Upper back pain occurs in the thoracic region, from the base of the neck to the lower ribs. It may feel like a knot between the shoulder blades, a broad ache after computer work, or pain with twisting and deep breathing. Because the upper back connects the neck, ribs, shoulders, and low back, discomfort in one area can alter movement elsewhere.

## Common Musculoskeletal Contributors

Long periods of sitting with the head and shoulders forward can overload the muscles that stabilize the shoulder blades. Repeated reaching, lifting, overhead activity, coughing, stress-related muscle guarding, and a sudden increase in exercise may also irritate the area. Tight chest muscles can pull the shoulders forward while overworked muscles along the spine work harder to hold the body upright.

A thorough assessment considers the location of pain, range of motion, work and training habits, prior injuries, and whether symptoms travel into the arm, chest, or neck.

## How RPT Clinic Approaches Upper Back Tension

Robotic Precision Therapy uses controlled, gentle, targeted pressure to address areas of muscular tightness identified during an assessment. For upper back symptoms, care may focus on muscles around the shoulder blades, chest, neck, and thoracic spine. The aim is to support comfortable movement and reduce protective muscle guarding as part of a broader plan that can include mobility work and gradual return to activity.

## When to Seek Medical Care

Seek urgent care for upper back pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath, fainting, sweating, fever, unexplained weight loss, or symptoms after a significant fall or collision. Persistent pain, numbness, weakness, or pain that disrupts sleep also warrants evaluation.
