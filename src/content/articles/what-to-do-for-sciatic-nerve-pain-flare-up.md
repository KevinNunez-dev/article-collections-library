## Sciatic nerve pain flaring up? Start here

First, check whether you have symptoms that need immediate medical attention. If not, stop forcing the activity that clearly aggravates the pain and try a comfortable change of position.

The goal is not to push through a dramatic stretch until something “releases.” It is to avoid making symptoms worse while deciding whether self-care or an evaluation is appropriate.

Sciatica can involve irritation of the nerves supplying the leg. A video promising instant pain relief cannot identify the cause of your symptoms.

## Step 1: Check for symptoms that should not wait

Seek emergency care for:

- New difficulty urinating or loss of bladder or bowel control.
- Numbness around the genitals, anus, or inner thighs.
- Sudden leg weakness or numbness.
- Severe or worsening weakness or numbness in both legs.
- Symptoms following a serious accident.

These are not situations for stretching, massage, or waiting for a routine appointment. Read [when to see a doctor for sciatic nerve pain](/health/when-to-see-a-doctor-for-sciatic-nerve-pain/) for additional guidance.

The [NHS sciatica guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sciatica/) explains the emergency signs and when to arrange an assessment.

## Step 2: Stop testing the painful movement

If a deep forward bend, twist, or stretch repeatedly triggers sciatic nerve pain, do not keep repeating it to check whether the problem has disappeared.

Move out of the position gradually. Try another comfortable position rather than searching for one posture that everyone should use.

Stop an exercise if symptoms worsen, spread farther down the leg, or include new numbness or weakness. A clinician can help determine whether that exercise belongs in your plan.

## Step 3: Try comfortable movement rather than prolonged bed rest

Short, tolerable periods of movement may be more manageable than a long exercise session. Depending on your symptoms, that might mean a brief walk or regularly changing between sitting and standing.

Do not turn “stay active” into an instruction to ignore worsening pain. Reduce or stop activities that aggravate symptoms and seek advice when ordinary movement becomes difficult.

The [Mayo Clinic treatment guidance for back pain](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/back-pain/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20369911) recommends light activity as tolerated rather than prolonged avoidance of movement.

If you are considering medication, ask a pharmacist or healthcare professional what is appropriate for your medical history and other medicines.

## Step 4: Do not assume the problem is one tight or weak muscle

Pain around the low back, hip, or buttock can make it tempting to blame a tight piriformis or weak back muscles.

Those may be considerations, but they do not explain every case. Daily back pain with leg symptoms needs a broader look when it persists or keeps returning.

For an explanation of overlapping patterns, read [how to tell sciatica from piriformis-related pain](/health/how-to-tell-sciatica-from-piriformis-pain/).

## Step 5: Arrange care if the pattern is not improving

Contact a healthcare professional when symptoms are severe, worsening, persistent, or limiting normal activities.

If you consult a back pain specialist, explain what changed during the flare-up. Mention new leg symptoms, reduced walking tolerance, or difficulty lifting the foot—not just where the back hurts.

Bring details of which movements helped and which made symptoms worse. That information can help guide an individualized plan.
