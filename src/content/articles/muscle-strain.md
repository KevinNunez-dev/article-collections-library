## What Is a Pulled Muscle?

A **pulled muscle** is the everyday name for a **muscle strain**: muscle fibers that were stretched too far or partially torn. It often happens during sprinting, jumping, lifting, or sudden changes of direction, and it commonly affects the hamstrings, calves, groin, and low back.

## Pulled Muscle vs. Strain vs. Tear vs. Spasm vs. Sprain

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Pulled muscle | Everyday term for a muscle strain |
| Strain | Overstretched or partially torn muscle or tendon fibers |
| Tear | A more severe strain, partial or complete |
| Spasm | Involuntary muscle tightening, often protective after a strain |
| Sprain | Injury to a ligament, not a muscle |

See [muscle spasms](/health/muscle-spasms) for why the muscle may tighten after injury.

## Strain Grades and Symptoms

- **Grade 1 (mild):** mild pain, tightness, minimal swelling, near-normal strength.
- **Grade 2 (moderate):** noticeable pain, swelling, possible bruising, reduced strength and motion.
- **Grade 3 (severe):** severe pain, a pop, major bruising, marked weakness, or inability to use the muscle. Needs medical evaluation.

## Pulled Muscle Symptoms

- Sudden or gradual pain during activity
- Tenderness and stiffness
- Swelling or bruising
- Pain when the muscle is stretched or contracted
- Weakness or a feeling the muscle "gave way"

## Common Locations

- **Hamstring and quadriceps:** sprinting, kicking
- **Calf:** push-off, sudden stops (see [calf pain](/health/calf-pain))
- **Groin:** cutting, kicking (see [groin pain](/health/groin-pain))
- **Low back:** lifting, twisting (see [lower back pain](/health/lower-back-pain))
- **Neck and shoulder:** sudden movements, poor sleep position

## Safe Self-Care in the First Days

1. Reduce activities that provoke pain.
2. Use cold or warmth, whichever eases pain.
3. Keep moving gently within comfort.
4. Avoid aggressive stretching or deep pressure on a fresh strain.
5. Gradually return to walking and light activity.

## Recovery Timeline

Mild strains often settle in one to three weeks, moderate strains in several weeks, and severe tears may take months. Timelines vary, and returning too soon is a common reason for re-injury.

## How Physical Therapy Helps

A therapist screens severity, then builds a graded plan: gentle movement, progressive strengthening, mobility, and sport- or work-specific return criteria. When appropriate, RPT Clinic can use gentle, targeted Robotic Precision Therapy pressure around the injured area as tissue tolerance allows. Persistent weakness is addressed through [muscle weakness](/health/muscle-weakness) strategies, and lingering tightness through [muscle tightness](/health/muscle-tightness) and [muscle stiffness](/health/muscle-stiffness) guidance.
