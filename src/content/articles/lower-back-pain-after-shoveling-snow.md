## Why does my lower back hurt after shoveling snow?

Snow shoveling combines repeated pushing, lifting, and turning. After a Michigan snowfall, an unusually long or strenuous session can leave your lower back sore or aggravate an existing problem. A muscle strain is one possibility, but back pain has several causes and cannot be diagnosed from the activity alone.

If soreness persists after the snow is cleared, RPT Clinic can discuss whether an assessment is appropriate. After assessment, Robotic Precision Therapy may use the RX2600 Therapeutic Robot to apply pressure to selected soft tissues when muscular contributors are suitable to address.

## Is it normal to be sore after shoveling?

An ache or stiffness after unfamiliar activity can occur, particularly if you spent a long time clearing heavy snow. Notice where you feel it and whether everyday movements are becoming easier or harder. Avoid assuming that severe pain, pain after a fall, or pain that travels into a leg is ordinary soreness.

Stop shoveling if pain is sharp, worsening, or limiting your movement. Ask someone else to finish the job. See [back pain](/health/back-pain/) and [when to see a doctor for back pain](/health/when-to-see-a-doctor-for-back-pain/) for more assessment questions.

## What can you do for a sore back after shoveling?

For mild, uncomplicated soreness, consider:

- Pausing the activity that made the pain worse and changing position as needed.
- Moving gently within your comfort level instead of forcing painful stretches.
- Using a comfortable supported position and returning gradually to usual activities as tolerated.
- Noting whether pain is improving, spreading, or interfering with walking, sleep, or daily tasks.

These are general steps, not a diagnosis. If pain is severe, getting worse, or not improving, ask a healthcare professional what evaluation and treatment are appropriate for you. If symptoms travel down your leg, see [sciatica](/health/sciatica/).

## How can you reduce the strain of snow removal?

First consider whether shoveling is safe for you. The [American Heart Association](https://newsroom.heart.org/local-news/shoveling-snow-can-be-dangerous-in-extreme-cold) advises people with known or suspected heart disease or risk factors for heart disease to have someone else remove their snow.

If shoveling is appropriate for you:

1. Start gradually, pace yourself, and take breaks.
2. Push or sweep snow rather than lifting and throwing it when possible.
3. Use small loads if lifting is necessary, and turn your feet rather than twisting while carrying snow.
4. Dress for the weather and choose footwear with good traction. Ask for help with heavy snow or icy conditions.
5. Stop immediately for chest discomfort, unusual breathlessness, lightheadedness, or other concerning symptoms.

These steps cannot guarantee that you will avoid injury or heart strain. Snow blowers also require care and can cause injuries; follow the manufacturer's safety instructions.

## When should you seek urgent care?

**Call 911** for chest pain or pressure, shortness of breath, fainting, or other possible heart attack symptoms. Do not assume chest, arm, back, or jaw discomfort during exertion is a muscle strain.

Seek emergency care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or saddle area, or rapidly worsening leg weakness. Severe pain after a fall or major injury also needs urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for an RPT appointment.
