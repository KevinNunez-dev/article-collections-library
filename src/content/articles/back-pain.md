## What can cause back pain?

Back pain can feel achy, stiff, sharp, or burning. It may follow an injury, develop gradually, or recur without a clear trigger. Muscles and joints may contribute, but nerve irritation and other medical conditions can also cause pain. Persistent pain lasting more than 12 weeks is often called chronic; that label does not identify its cause.

## What should you discuss at an assessment?

Explain where the pain is, when it began, whether it travels into a leg, and what affects sitting, walking, sleep, or work. Mention any injury, previous treatment, imaging, numbness, or weakness. A clinician can discuss what assessment is appropriate and whether you need medical input.

Activity and exercise, education, medications, and other treatments can all have a role depending on the person. Do not stop prescribed treatment without discussing it with your treating clinician. See [physical therapy versus RPT for lower back pain](/health/physical-therapy-vs-robotic-precision-therapy-lower-back-pain/).

## How does Robotic Precision Therapy fit into back pain care?

After assessment, RPT Clinic may use the RX2600 Therapeutic Robot to apply pressure to selected soft tissues when muscular contributors appear appropriate to address. RPT does not diagnose the cause of pain, repair or reposition discs, or guarantee an outcome. Ask how treatment fits your goals and when to reconsider the plan if it is not helping.

## When should you seek urgent care?

Seek emergency care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or saddle area, or rapidly worsening leg weakness. Seek urgent medical assessment for severe pain after major trauma or fever with severe back pain. Persistent or changing symptoms also warrant assessment.
