## Who should you see for TMJ or jaw pain near Detroit?

The temporomandibular joints, often called TMJ, connect the jaw to the skull. Temporomandibular disorders can involve the joints, surrounding muscles, or both. Jaw pain can also have dental or other causes, so a dentist or primary care clinician is often a useful starting point.

Depending on the findings, care may involve an orofacial pain specialist, oral and maxillofacial specialist, or physical therapist. Ask how the provider distinguishes jaw-joint or muscle symptoms from a tooth problem and when another opinion is needed. Read our [TMJ guide](/health/tmj) and the [National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research overview](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/tmd).

## What should an evaluation cover?

Describe where the pain is felt and whether chewing, speaking, yawning, or waking in the morning changes it. Mention jaw clicking, locking, difficulty opening, recent dental work, injury, headaches, and symptoms around the ear. Bring details of previous dental or medical assessments.

A clinician may assess jaw movement, tenderness, relevant neck movement, and daily function. Clicking alone without pain or difficulty is not always a reason for treatment. Ask which findings need dental or medical follow-up before beginning a therapy plan.

## When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent assessment for a jaw that locks after injury, facial swelling with fever, or trouble breathing or swallowing. Jaw discomfort with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, or other possible heart symptoms is an emergency: call 911. Persistent dental pain, worsening swelling, or difficulty opening the mouth also warrants prompt dental or medical advice.

## Questions before choosing care

- Is a dental examination needed to rule out a tooth or gum problem?
- What is contributing to the pain or restricted movement?
- What everyday changes are reasonable while symptoms are assessed?
- What benefits and limitations should I expect from the proposed care?
- How will we measure chewing, speaking, or opening comfortably, and when will we reassess?

Avoid claims that one device or treatment fixes every jaw problem. Coordinate care with your dentist or physician when appropriate.
