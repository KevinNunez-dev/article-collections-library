## The short answer

Some health plans cover eligible physical therapy for sciatica symptoms, but **coverage is not automatic**. Your specific service, clinician, network, plan benefits, and any referral or authorization rules matter. Ask the insurer and the provider about your situation before relying on an estimate.

Sciatica describes pain that may travel from the lower back into the leg. It is not, by itself, a diagnosis or proof that a particular service is covered. For symptom guidance, read [sciatica care near Detroit](/health/sciatica-relief-near-detroit-rpt).

## Check benefits for the actual provider and service

Have your plan card and the provider's details available. Ask:

1. Is this provider in my network for the proposed service?
2. Is the service covered under my plan, and is a referral or prior authorization required?
3. What deductible remains, and what copay or coinsurance may apply?
4. Is there a visit limit or a shared limit with other therapies?
5. What can change the estimate after a claim is reviewed?

Ask the provider for the service description and available codes. Write down the date, representative's name, and reference number for your call. A benefit estimate is not a claim decision. See the more detailed [lower back pain insurance checklist](/health/lower-back-pain-therapy-insurance-questions).

For general definitions of deductibles and other plan costs, see [HealthCare.gov's total-cost guide](https://www.healthcare.gov/choose-a-plan/your-total-costs/).

## Compare the cost of a plan, not just coverage

Coverage can still leave you with a deductible, copay, coinsurance, or uncovered services. An out-of-pocket price may be due before a claim is decided. Compare written quotes, the proposed care, and expected travel and time costs using the [sciatica copay-versus-cash guide](/health/sciatica-treatment-cost-copay-vs-cash). Coverage or cost alone does not determine which care is clinically appropriate.

## When to seek urgent help

Seek urgent medical care for new or worsening leg weakness, saddle or groin numbness, new loss of bladder or bowel control, or severe pain after major trauma. Do not delay emergency assessment to resolve an insurance question.
