## Migraine Relief: Stop Headaches Before They Start

Migraines affect 12% of the global population and can be severely debilitating. Migraine pain often radiates from the base of the skull, temples, or across the head, sometimes with vision changes, nausea, or sensitivity to light and sound.

**But here's what most people don't realize: Most migraines stem from tight neck and jaw muscles compressing nerves, not from neurological dysfunction.**

At **RPT Clinic**, we use the **RX2600 Therapeutic Robot** to release tight suboccipital and cervical muscles (base of skull) that compress cranial nerves and restrict blood flow to the brain. Many patients experience significant relief within 3-8 sessions.

## Understanding Migraines

The head and neck region is an intricate network of skull, cervical spine, jaw, and 20+ muscles. When muscles become tight and shortened from stress, poor posture, or teeth grinding, they:
- Compress cranial nerves
- Restrict blood flow to the brain
- Create muscle referred pain patterns
- Trigger migraine cascade

## Common Migraine Symptoms

- Severe throbbing head pain (usually one-sided)
- Visual disturbances (aura, flashing lights)
- Nausea, vomiting, or sensitivity to light/sound
- Neck stiffness or shoulder tension
- Fatigue and difficulty concentrating
- Pain worse with physical activity
- Recurring headaches at regular intervals

## The Root Cause: Cervicogenic Dysfunction

**Most migraines stem from cervicogenic dysfunction**—muscle imbalance in the neck:

**Tight Suboccipital Muscles** (base of skull) compress the greater occipital nerve, triggering occipital neuralgia and migraines

**Tight Upper Trapezius** pulls the head forward, restricting blood flow and compressing cervical nerves

**Forward Head Posture** from computer work creates muscle imbalance throughout the head and neck

**Stress and Teeth Grinding** tighten jaw muscles, creating referred pain to the temples and entire head

**Poor Neck Mechanics** from prolonged sitting create protective muscle spasm

## Why Migraine Medications Plateau

**Pain medications** mask migraines but don't address muscle compression  
**Preventive medications** reduce frequency but underlying muscle tension remains  
**Botox injections** provide temporary relief  
**Massage** feels good temporarily but doesn't achieve lasting muscle lengthening  
**Manual PT** struggles to access deep suboccipital muscles
