## Are physical therapy and surgery alternatives for sciatica?

Sometimes a medical team discusses non-surgical care and surgery as options; in other cases surgery needs prompt consideration, or physical therapy is part of recovery after a procedure. Sciatica is a symptom pattern, not a diagnosis. Whether either option is appropriate depends on the cause, examination, symptoms, and your clinician's advice.

Do not delay a recommended medical or surgical evaluation to try RPT or make a cost comparison. The [AAOS sciatica overview](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/sciatica/) explains general treatment options.

## Compare estimates with the same questions

| Question | Physical therapy | Surgery or procedure |
|---|---|---|
| What is included? | Evaluation, follow-ups, and any separately billed services | Ask about surgeon, facility, anesthesia, imaging, medication, and follow-up |
| What might I owe? | Get a provider quote and plan-specific insurance estimate | Ask all relevant providers and your insurer for a procedure-specific estimate |
| What time is involved? | Appointment, travel, and any missed work | Ask the surgical team about restrictions, rehabilitation, and return to work |
| How is progress reviewed? | Ask about goals, reassessment, and referral | Ask about follow-up, expected milestones, and whom to contact about concerns |

These are different services and may not produce the same outcomes. A surgery price is not automatically your insured responsibility, and a therapy copay is not necessarily the complete visit cost. Include possible follow-up therapy after surgery where recommended. Keep travel and unpaid work separate from medical bills.

## Ask about recovery, not a generic timeline

Ask your surgeon what procedure is being considered, what it is intended to address, the potential benefits and risks, and how your own health and work may affect recovery. Ask what restrictions, help at home, follow-up, and rehabilitation might be needed. Recovery varies; no single number of days applies to everyone. An estimate is not a guarantee.

For a therapy plan, ask what the first evaluation includes, what activities you can do, how progress will be measured, and what would trigger referral or a change in care. ## When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent medical care for new or worsening leg weakness, numbness in the groin or saddle area, new loss of bladder or bowel control, or severe pain after major trauma. Do not postpone care while comparing costs.
