## Runner's Knee Relief: Return to Pain-Free Running

Runner's knee (patellofemoral pain syndrome) affects 50% of distance runners and causes anterior knee pain, especially with running downhill, stairs, or prolonged walking. It's the most common running injury.

**The root cause: 90% of runner's knee involves specific muscle imbalances where tight quadriceps muscles pull the kneecap sideways and weak glute muscles fail to stabilize the knee.**

At **RPT Clinic**, we use the **RX2600 Therapeutic Robot** to release tight quads and enable proper glute activation. Most runners notice improvement within 6-8 sessions and return to pain-free running.

## Understanding Runner's Knee

Runner's knee is patellofemoral pain syndrome—pain behind or around the kneecap. It develops from:
- Tight quadriceps muscles pulling kneecap sideways
- Weak hip and glute muscles failing to stabilize knee
- Hip adductor tightness pulling knee inward
- Tight calf muscles altering ankle mechanics
- Poor running form compensation
- Training load spikes

## Common Runner's Knee Symptoms

- Dull, aching pain behind or around kneecap
- Pain worse with running, especially downhill
- Pain worse with stairs or squatting
- Pain during prolonged sitting ("movie theater sign")
- Morning stiffness
- Swelling around kneecap
- Popping or creaking sensation
- Pain that worsens with increased mileage

## The Root Challenge: Quad Tightness + Glute Weakness

Runner's knee stems from a specific muscle imbalance:

**Tight Quadriceps** pull the kneecap sideways, causing tracking problems and cartilage pain

**Weak Hip Abductors (Glutes)** fail to stabilize the pelvis during running, allowing the knee to cave inward

**Tight Hip Adductors** pull the knee medially, worsening tracking

**Limited Ankle Mobility** from tight calves alters running mechanics

**Poor Running Form** from these imbalances compounds the problem

## Why Rest and Therapy Plateau

**Rest Works Temporarily**: But doesn't address muscle imbalance, so pain returns

**Standard PT**: May not adequately address quad tightness preventing glute activation

**Orthotics**: Provide symptom support but don't fix root cause

**Strengthening Alone**: Difficult to activate weak glutes with tight quads resisting activation
