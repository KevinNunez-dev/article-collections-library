## Who treats hip pain near Detroit?

A hip pain specialist may be a primary care clinician, sports medicine physician, orthopedic specialist, or physical therapist. If symptoms followed a fall or you cannot put weight on the leg, medical assessment comes first. For persistent non-emergency pain, a clinician can help decide whether rehabilitation, imaging, or another evaluation is appropriate.

People use the word hip to describe pain in the groin, outside of the hip, buttock, or lower back. Each area provides a clue, but none proves the source. Our [hip pain guide](/health/hip-pain) and [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003179.htm) explain common patterns.

## What should an evaluation include?

Describe where the pain is most noticeable, how long it has been present, and whether it followed an injury. Mention pain when walking, climbing stairs, getting in a car, or lying on one side. Tell the clinician about clicking, restricted motion, numbness, and any relevant health history.

An examination may include hip movement and strength, gait, balance, and a look at the back or other nearby areas when appropriate. Ask whether the pattern suggests a hip joint problem, surrounding soft-tissue problem, or another source. See [hip pain versus groin pain](/health/hip-pain-vs-groin-pain).

## When to seek medical care

Seek urgent medical assessment if you cannot bear weight after a fall or have a hot, swollen joint with fever. Sudden severe hip pain without a clear cause also needs prompt medical advice. Progressive weakness, unexplained weight loss, or pain that consistently wakes you should be discussed with a physician.

## Questions to ask a hip pain provider

- What did the examination show, and are other causes being considered?
- Would imaging or a physician referral change the next step?
- Which daily activities should I modify, and what can I continue?
- How will walking, sleep, or another goal be tracked?
- When should we reassess or change the plan?
