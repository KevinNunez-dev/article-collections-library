## Can disc and nerve pain be treated without surgery?

Many people with a herniated disc or nerve-related symptoms explore non-surgical care. That can be a reasonable option, but the right plan depends on the cause, severity, examination, and changes over time. Some symptoms require urgent medical attention, and some people benefit from a surgical opinion.

A bulging or herniated disc on an MRI is not automatically the source of pain. Disc changes may also appear in people without symptoms. A clinician should compare the imaging, if available, with where symptoms travel and what the examination shows. The [AAOS guide to a herniated disc in the lower back](https://www.orthoinfo.org/bodypart/back/herniated-disk-in-the-lower-back/) describes symptoms and treatment options.

## What should an assessment cover?

Explain where pain starts, whether it travels into an arm or leg, and whether you have numbness, tingling, or weakness. Bring previous reports and treatment information if available. Depending on the symptoms, a clinician may assess movement, strength, sensation, reflexes, and walking.

Ask what findings suggest nerve involvement, whether the disc finding matches your symptoms, and whether medical evaluation or imaging is appropriate. See our [herniated disc specialist guide](/health/herniated-disc-specialist-detroit) and [bulging disc overview](/health/bulging-disc).

## Non-surgical options to discuss

Depending on your diagnosis and goals, a plan may include:

- Activity changes and movement you can tolerate while symptoms are assessed.
- Physical therapy addressing function, movement, and a gradual return to activities.
- Medication advice from a qualified clinician who can review your health history.
- Injections or other medical options for selected situations, discussed with a physician.

Ask what each option is intended to do, what its limitations are, and when to reassess. Relief is not guaranteed, and an approach that helps one person may not fit another. Our [non-surgical sciatica guide](/health/non-surgical-sciatica-pain-relief-michigan) focuses on pain that travels into the leg.

## When to seek urgent care or a specialist opinion

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the saddle or groin area, rapidly worsening weakness, or severe pain after major trauma. New or worsening arm or leg weakness also warrants prompt medical assessment.

If symptoms remain disabling, worsen, or do not improve with appropriate care, ask a physician whether a spine specialist or surgical opinion is needed. A consultation helps you understand options; it does not commit you to an operation. Do not delay recommended medical care to pursue a particular treatment.
