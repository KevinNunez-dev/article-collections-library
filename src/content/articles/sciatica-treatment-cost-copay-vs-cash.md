## Compare what you may actually pay

When sciatica symptoms disrupt sitting, walking, or sleep, a low advertised copay can look easier to budget than a cash price. But a copay may not be the whole bill, and an out-of-pocket quote may include services differently. Ask what is clinically appropriate, obtain written estimates, and compare the full plan rather than the first price you see.

## A simple example—not a treatment recommendation

Suppose an insurance-based provider estimates **$40 per visit for four visits**, and your insurer confirms that this copay applies to each of those visits with no additional deductible or charges. The illustrative total would be **$160**. Those assumptions may not apply to your plan; the actual claim can differ.

At RPT Clinic, the listed first visit is **$225 for 90 minutes**, including assessment and treatment, and each later 60-minute session is **$150**. One first visit and three follow-ups would total **$675 before any reimbursement**. Confirm current rates and terms. RPT Clinic does not bill insurance; an invoice with medical codes may be submitted, but reimbursement is not guaranteed.

Four visits are used only to make the arithmetic clear. The two services and their clinical outcomes are not assumed equivalent, and neither example predicts how many visits you need. Do not interpret the example as a claim that one choice saves money or works better.

| Deductible, copay, coinsurance | Ask insurer for the specific service | Ask whether any reimbursement is possible |
| Visits, authorization, or referral limits | Confirm with insurer | Confirm claim requirements if applicable |
| Travel, parking, and unpaid work | Estimate separately | Estimate separately |
| Deposits, cancellations, other charges | Ask provider | Ask provider |

Keep unpaid work separate from the visit price and avoid double counting it. If reimbursement is uncertain, budget the full amount first and treat a payment from your plan as uncertain until the claim is decided.

For definitions of deductibles, copays, and other plan costs, see [HealthCare.gov's total-cost guide](https://www.healthcare.gov/choose-a-plan/your-total-costs/).

## When not to wait for an estimate
