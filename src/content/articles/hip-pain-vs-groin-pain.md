## The short answer

**Hip pain is usually felt on the side of the hip or the buttock; groin pain is felt in the fold where the abdomen meets the thigh.** They overlap because problems in the hip joint often show up in the groin.

## A simple tour of the area

- **Hip joint:** a ball-and-socket joint deep in the pelvis.
- **Hip flexors:** muscles at the front of the hip that lift the knee.
- **Adductors:** inner-thigh muscles that attach near the groin.
- **Glutes:** buttock muscles that stabilize the hip.
- **Inguinal region:** the groin crease, where hernias can occur.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Feature | Hip-type pain | Groin-type pain |
|---|---|---|
| Typical location | Side of hip, buttock, upper thigh | Crease at the front, inner thigh |
| Common triggers | Lying on the side, stairs, long sitting | Walking, kicking, changing direction |
| Muscle contributors | Glutes, hip rotators, tensor fasciae latae | Adductors, hip flexors |
| Joint clues | Stiffness, reduced rotation | Deep pinching, catching |
| Non-muscle causes | Bursitis, arthritis | Hernia, joint problems |

## Why they get confused

The hip joint is deep and pain from it is often felt in the groin or even the knee. Meanwhile, tight muscles around the hip can limit movement and create pain in both areas. Lower back problems can add to the picture.

## What to tell a clinician

1. Where it hurts, by pointing
2. What movements provoke it
3. Whether it is stiff, catches, or gives way
4. What the pain is like at night
5. Any injury, sport, or recent change in activity

## When to seek urgent care

- A painful groin bulge that will not go back in
- Testicular pain or swelling
- Fever with hip or groin pain
- Sudden severe pain, or inability to bear weight
- Numbness in the groin or saddle area, or loss of bladder or bowel control
