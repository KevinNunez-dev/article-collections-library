## What is nerve pain, and what can cause it?

Nerve-related pain can feel burning, shooting, tingling, or electric and may occur with numbness or weakness. Sciatica is one example of pain that travels along a nerve pathway, but similar symptoms can have different causes. A symptom description alone cannot identify the source; a clinician considers your history and examination. See the [Mayo Clinic overview of sciatica symptoms and causes](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/sciatica/symptoms-causes/syc-20377435).

## What treatments may be discussed for nerve pain?

Care depends on the cause, severity, and your health history. A clinician may discuss activity changes, physical therapy, medication, further testing, or referral to a medical or spine specialist. Some conditions may lead to discussion of electrical nerve stimulation, but that is a different treatment from soft-tissue therapy. Ask what a proposed treatment is intended to address, what alternatives exist, and when progress should be reviewed.

## Does electrical stimulation help sciatica?

Electrical stimulation can refer to different treatments, from surface electrodes to peripheral nerve stimulation procedures. Whether a specific method is appropriate depends on the diagnosis and the evidence for that treatment. Ask the treating clinician what device is proposed, what it is intended to do, and what alternatives are available. RPT's pressure-based therapy is not electrical stimulation.

If you are specifically looking for an electrical nerve stimulator, contact the clinic to confirm whether it offers that service or ask your physician about an appropriate provider.

## How is Robotic Precision Therapy different from nerve stimulation?

Robotic Precision Therapy applies pressure to selected soft tissues; peripheral nerve stimulation uses electrical impulses delivered near a nerve. These are not interchangeable treatments. Read the [Cleveland Clinic overview of peripheral nerve stimulation](https://www.clevelandclinicabudhabi.ae/en/health-library/health-resources/treatments-and-procedures/peripheral-nerve-stimulation) for a description of that electrical procedure.

After assessment, RPT may address muscular contributors around the low back, hips, or glutes when appropriate. It does not diagnose nerve disease, release a compressed nerve, repair a disc, or guarantee relief. See [non-surgical disc and nerve pain care in Michigan](/health/non-surgical-disc-nerve-pain-relief-michigan) for broader treatment considerations.

## Can RPT Clinic help with sciatica or radiating leg pain?

RPT may be considered only when the assessment identifies appropriate muscular contributors. Sciatica can have causes that need medical evaluation; RPT is not a substitute for that evaluation or for specialist care when indicated. Compare [sciatica treatment options in Troy](/health/sciatica-treatment-troy-mi) and [when to see a doctor for sciatic nerve pain](/health/when-to-see-a-doctor-for-sciatic-nerve-pain).

## Which nerve-pain symptoms need urgent medical attention?

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or saddle area, or sudden or worsening leg weakness. Severe pain after major trauma also needs prompt assessment. The [JAMA patient guide to sciatica](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2811335) explains warning symptoms that should be assessed promptly. Do not wait for a routine therapy appointment when urgent symptoms are present.
