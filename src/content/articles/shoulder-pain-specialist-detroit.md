## Who should you see for shoulder pain near Detroit?

A shoulder pain specialist may be a sports medicine physician, orthopedic specialist, primary care clinician, or physical therapist. The right choice depends on whether the problem followed an injury, how much movement or strength you have lost, and whether a medical evaluation is needed.

Pain reaching overhead, difficulty putting on a coat, and discomfort at night are useful clues, but none proves a particular diagnosis. A careful examination helps distinguish shoulder problems from symptoms that may come from the neck or elsewhere. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003171.htm) discusses possible causes and warning signs.

## What a shoulder assessment should include

Describe how the symptoms began and where you feel them. Mention any fall, lifting incident, prior surgery, tingling, or weakness. A clinician may assess:

- Active and assisted shoulder movement and the positions that reproduce pain.
- Strength and function during reaching, lifting, or other relevant tasks.
- Neck movement and arm sensation when the symptom pattern calls for it.
- The effect on sleep, work, sports, and daily activities.

Ask what the findings suggest, whether imaging is likely to change the plan, and how progress will be measured. Read more about [shoulder pain](/health/shoulder-pain) and [shoulder blade pain](/health/shoulder-blade-pain).

## When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent medical assessment for a visibly deformed shoulder or inability to move the arm after a significant injury. New substantial weakness also deserves prompt assessment. Shoulder or arm discomfort with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, or other possible heart symptoms is an emergency: call 911.

## Choosing a treatment plan

Depending on the diagnosis, care may include activity changes, rehabilitation, medication advice from an appropriate clinician, or an orthopedic opinion. Ask what to do if lifting or exercise increases pain, when to reassess, and which findings would change the plan. A provider should not promise that one treatment works for every shoulder problem.
