## The short answer

**Robotic Precision Therapy (RPT) is a hands-free treatment approach in which a clinician uses the RX2600 Therapeutic Robot to apply controlled, repeatable pressure to muscles.** The aim is to address muscular contributors to pain and stiffness that an assessment suggests are relevant.

That is the headline. The sections below break it down so you can decide whether it is worth a closer look. Open any question to get a fast answer.

## Quick answers before you read on

<summary style="cursor:pointer;font-weight:600">Is a robot doing the treatment on its own?</summary>

No. A clinician evaluates you first, decides where and how the treatment should be applied, and oversees the session.

<details style="border:1px solid #d7dee6;border-radius:8px;padding:.75rem 1rem;margin:.6rem 0">
<summary style="cursor:pointer;font-weight:600">What problems is it used for?</summary>

It is used for muscle-related pain and tightness, such as in the back, neck, hips, and legs. It is not a diagnosis, and it is not appropriate for every cause of pain.

</details>

## How it works, step by step

1. **You describe your symptoms.** When did they start, what makes them better or worse, and what have you already tried?
2. **A clinician assesses you.** This looks at movement, posture, and where muscles feel tight or tender.
3. **You decide together whether RPT fits.** If another type of care seems better, that should be said plainly.
4. **The robot applies pressure to chosen areas.** The clinician sets the location and pressure and can change them during the session.
5. **Your response is reviewed.** What changed, what did not, and what to do next.

## What the RX2600 does differently from hands-on work

| Feature | Hands-on therapy | Robotic Precision Therapy |
|---|---|---|
| Who applies pressure | A therapist's hands | The RX2600, directed by a clinician |
| Consistency | Varies with the person and the day | Pressure can be set and repeated |
| Therapist fatigue | Can limit long sustained pressure | Not a limiting factor for the device |
| Assessment | Required | Required before treatment |

Neither approach is automatically better for everyone. The right choice depends on your symptoms and what the assessment finds.

## Who may want to ask about RPT

People often ask about RPT when they have:

- Back, neck, or shoulder tightness that keeps returning
- Hip, glute, or leg pain with muscle tension
- Stiffness after an injury has otherwise settled
- Pain that has not responded the way they hoped to other care

If your situation sounds similar, [Back Pain](/health/back-pain), [Hip Pain](/health/hip-pain), and [Sciatica](/health/sciatica) explain how those conditions are commonly approached.

## Where it is not the right first step

RPT is not a replacement for emergency or medical care. Get urgent help for:

- Sudden severe pain after a fall or accident
- Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin or saddle area
- New weakness in a leg or arm
- Chest pain, shortness of breath, fever with severe pain, or an unexplained swelling and redness in one leg
