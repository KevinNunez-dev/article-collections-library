## Tennis Elbow Relief: Restore Full Arm Function

Tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis) causes pain on the outside of the elbow, especially with gripping, lifting, or repetitive arm movement. It affects tennis players, desk workers, painters, plumbers, and anyone with repetitive forearm use.

**The root cause: 90% of tennis elbow involves tight forearm extensor muscles concentrating excessive force on the lateral epicondyle where they attach.**

At **RPT Clinic**, we use the **RX2600 Therapeutic Robot** to release forearm muscle tension and restore arm balance. Most patients notice improvement within 4-6 sessions and return to full activity.

## Understanding Tennis Elbow

Tennis elbow develops from muscle imbalance and repetitive strain:
- Tight forearm extensors create excessive tension on attachment point
- Weak forearm muscles fail to distribute load properly
- Poor grip mechanics from tight muscles
- Repetitive gripping or impact increases strain
- Inadequate recovery allows inflammation to persist
- Muscle tightness in shoulder and neck compounds arm dysfunction

## Common Tennis Elbow Symptoms

- Pain on outside of elbow
- Pain worse with gripping or lifting
- Pain worse with repetitive arm movement
- Pain worsening gradually (not sudden onset)
- Weak grip strength
- Swelling on lateral elbow
- Morning stiffness or soreness
- Pain radiating down forearm
- Difficulty holding objects

## The Root Challenge: Forearm Extensor Tightness + Weakness

Tennis elbow results from specific forearm imbalance:

**Tight Forearm Extensors** (extensor carpi radialis brevis, extensor carpi radialis longus) create excessive force at lateral epicondyle

**Weak Forearm Extensors** fail to distribute load during activity

**Tight Shoulder and Neck Muscles** alter arm mechanics increasing forearm strain

**Poor Grip Mechanics** from tight forearms increase elbow stress

**Repetitive Demands** without adequate recovery overwhelm already-imbalanced muscles

## Why Anti-inflammatory Treatment Plateaus

**Anti-inflammatories Mask Symptoms**: But muscle imbalance remains, so tennis elbow recurs

**Corticosteroid Injections**: Provide temporary relief but don't fix root cause

**Bracing**: Supports but doesn't resolve muscle tension

**Standard PT**: May not adequately address forearm muscle tightness
