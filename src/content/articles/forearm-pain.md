## What Causes Pain in the Forearm?

**Pain in the forearm** can feel like an ache, a burn, a tight band, or a sharp pain with gripping. The forearm contains many muscles and tendons that move the wrist and fingers, so it is easily overloaded by repetitive work, lifting, and sport. Pain can also be referred from the elbow, wrist, shoulder, or neck.

Common contributors:

- Repetitive strain from typing, mouse use, tools, or assembly work
- Grip overuse in climbing, weightlifting, or racquet sports
- Tendon irritation near the elbow or wrist
- Muscle strain or tension in the forearm belly
- Nerve irritation at the neck, elbow, or wrist
- Sudden increases in workload

## Forearm Pain by Location

### Pain Inside the Forearm

**Pain inside the forearm** is felt on the palm side. It often relates to the wrist and finger flexor muscles that work with gripping, carrying, and golf-type motions. It may extend toward the inner elbow. See [golfer's elbow](/health/golfers-elbow).

### Top Forearm Pain

**Top forearm pain** is on the back of the forearm, over the wrist and finger extensors. These muscles work during typing, mouse use, racquet sports, and lifting. See [tennis elbow](/health/tennis-elbow).

### Pain in the Mid Forearm

**Pain in mid forearm** often sits in the muscle belly and may follow heavy repetitive use. Pain after a direct blow, with swelling, or with visible deformity requires urgent evaluation.

### Pain in the Forearm Muscle and Aching Forearm

An **aching forearm** or **pain in the forearm muscle** often reflects fatigue, tension, and long periods of static gripping, such as holding a mouse, steering wheel, or tool.

| Location | Often involves | Commonly aggravated by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inside (palm side) | Wrist and finger flexors | Gripping, carrying, pulling |
| Top (back side) | Wrist and finger extensors | Typing, mouse, racquet sports |
| Mid forearm | Muscle belly, tendons | Repetitive heavy use |
| Whole forearm ache | Fatigue, tension | Long static positions |

## Repetitive Strain, Tendons, and Grip Overuse

Muscles and tendons need time to adapt to load. When tasks increase faster than tissue tolerance, irritation and pain can develop. Grip-heavy tasks and repetitive wrist motion are the common culprits. Pain that appears at the same point in a task and eases afterward often points to overuse.

## Wrist, Elbow, Shoulder, and Neck Connections

The forearm is part of an arm chain. A stiff neck, weak shoulder, irritated elbow tendon, or wrist problem can all change how the forearm is loaded. See [elbow pain](/health/elbow-pain), [wrist pain](/health/wrist-pain), [hand pain](/health/hand-pain), [shoulder pain](/health/shoulder-pain), and [neck pain](/health/neck-pain).

## Nerve Symptoms

Numbness, tingling, burning, or hand weakness can indicate nerve irritation at the neck, elbow, or wrist. These symptoms deserve an assessment. See [carpal tunnel syndrome](/health/carpal-tunnel-syndrome).

## Work and Ergonomic Use Cases

- **Desk and keyboard work:** keep wrists neutral, elbows near 90 degrees, and take micro-breaks every 30 to 45 minutes.
- **Mouse use:** keep the mouse close, use the whole arm rather than flicking the wrist, and consider a different mouse shape.
- **Tools and trades:** vary grip, use padded or larger handles, and rotate tasks.
- **Phone and tablet use:** avoid long periods with a bent wrist or elbow.
- **Driving:** avoid a tight, static grip on the wheel.

## Sport-Specific Use Cases

- **Racquet sports:** racquet grip size, string tension, and backhand mechanics matter.
- **Golf:** grip pressure and swing mechanics can load the inside forearm.
- **Climbing and weightlifting:** grip volume and progressive loading are key.
- **Cycling and rowing:** handlebar position and grip pressure affect the forearm.

## Safe Self-Care for Mild Pain

1. Reduce the aggravating task or break it into shorter blocks.
2. Use warmth before activity and cold after if it feels better.
3. Gently stretch the wrist flexors and extensors within comfort.
4. Adjust equipment and workstation setup.
5. Build back gradually as the pain settles.
