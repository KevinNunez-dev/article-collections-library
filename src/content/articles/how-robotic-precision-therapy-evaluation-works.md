## The short answer

A Robotic Precision Therapy evaluation is **a structured look at your history, your movement, and your muscles**, used to decide whether RPT appears appropriate and where treatment would focus. It is not a medical diagnosis.

## The five parts of an evaluation

### 1. History
Expect questions on when symptoms began, what changed, what helps, and what makes it worse. Previous injuries, surgeries, and medications matter too.

### 2. Red-flag screening
The clinician should ask about warning signs such as numbness, weakness, fever, unexplained weight loss, and changes in bladder or bowel control. These can change the plan.

### 3. Movement observation
You may be asked to stand, walk, bend, squat, or reach. The clinician watches for restrictions, compensations, and movements that reproduce your symptoms.

### 4. Muscle assessment
Hands-on assessment looks for tight, tender, or guarded muscles and how they relate to your symptoms. This is where the plan for the RX2600 Therapeutic Robot begins to take shape.

### 5. The plan discussion
You discuss the findings, whether RPT is suitable, how many visits might be reasonable, and how progress will be judged.

## What findings may mean

| Finding | What it may lead to |
|---|---|
| Muscular tension related to symptoms | A plan for RPT, reviewed as you respond |
| Mixed picture | RPT alongside other care, or a physician's input |
| Red-flag signs | Referral to a physician before treatment |
| Unclear cause | Further assessment or a second opinion |

## Questions to ask during the evaluation

- What did you find, in plain words?
- What do you think is contributing most?
- How will we measure progress?
- What would make you change the plan?
- What should I avoid or keep doing?

## When to see a physician first

Get medical care first for severe pain after trauma, new weakness, numbness in the groin or saddle area, loss of bladder or bowel control, fever with severe pain, or a painful, swollen leg.
