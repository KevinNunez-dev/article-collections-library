## The short answer

**Reviews are a useful starting point, not a verdict.** They can reveal how a clinic communicates and how appointments feel. They cannot tell you whether the treatment will work for your body.

## Quick answers

<summary style="cursor:pointer;font-weight:600">What is the best sign in a review?</summary>

Specifics: what the problem was, what the visit involved, how long it took, and how staff treated the person.

<details style="border:1px solid #d7dee6;border-radius:8px;padding:.75rem 1rem;margin:.6rem 0">
<summary style="cursor:pointer;font-weight:600">What is a warning sign?</summary>

Guarantees, "cured in one visit" language, or reviews that read like advertising.

</details>

<details style="border:1px solid #d7dee6;border-radius:8px;padding:.75rem 1rem;margin:.6rem 0">
<summary style="cursor:pointer;font-weight:600">What can reviews never tell me?</summary>

Whether your pain has the same cause as the reviewer's. Only an assessment can address that.

</details>

## What to look for in a review

- **The starting problem.** Is it similar to yours?
- **The process.** Was an assessment explained before treatment?
- **The timeline.** What changed, and when?
- **The people.** Were questions answered clearly?
- **The practical side.** Scheduling, cost clarity, and follow-up.

## What reviews cannot answer

| Question | Better source |
|---|---|
| Will it work for me? | A clinical evaluation |
| Is my pain from a muscle problem? | An assessment, and a physician if needed |
| What will it cost me? | The clinic and your insurer |
| How many visits will I need? | Your evaluation and how you respond |

## A 5-minute decision checklist

1. Have I read both positive and critical reviews?
2. Do I understand what the treatment is? See [what is Robotic Precision Therapy](/health/what-is-robotic-precision-therapy).
3. Do I know what the first visit involves? See the [first appointment guide](/health/first-robotic-precision-therapy-appointment).
4. Do I know the likely cost? See [cost and insurance questions](/health/robotic-precision-therapy-cost-insurance-questions).
5. Have I ruled out warning signs that need a physician first?

If you can answer yes to most, an evaluation is a reasonable next step.

## On patient stories and the team

Patient stories and team profiles help people decide. This library does not invent them. Any story shared should be real, and used with the patient's permission. Team information should come from the clinic. Ask the clinic who will assess and treat you.

## Seek urgent care instead if

You have severe pain after an injury, loss of bladder or bowel control, groin or saddle numbness, new weakness, chest pain, or a painful, swollen leg.
