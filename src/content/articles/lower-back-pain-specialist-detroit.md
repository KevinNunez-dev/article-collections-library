## Who should you see for lower back pain near Detroit?

A lower back pain specialist might be a primary care clinician, rehabilitation physician, spine specialist, or physical therapist. The right choice depends on your symptoms, medical history, and whether you need medical testing. For non-emergency pain that limits sitting, lifting, or walking, a primary care clinician or physical therapist can help identify a reasonable next step.

An evaluation matters more than a label. A responsible provider will explain when care is appropriate and when you need a physician or another specialist. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/backpain.html) provides an overview of back pain and treatment.

## What should the assessment cover?

Describe where the pain starts, whether it travels into a leg, and what happens when you bend, stand, sleep, or walk. Bring relevant records and a list of medicines and previous treatment. Your provider may assess:

- Back and hip movement and the activities that reproduce symptoms.
- Leg strength, sensation, walking, and other neurological signs when appropriate.
- The effect on work, exercise, sleep, and everyday tasks.
- Health history and warning signs that change the plan.

Pain can have different contributors. A scan is not automatically needed for every episode, and an image finding alone does not establish the cause. See our [lower back pain guide](/health/lower-back-pain) and [sciatica specialist guide](/health/sciatica-specialist-detroit) for leg symptoms.

## When to seek medical care urgently

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the saddle or groin area, rapidly worsening leg weakness, or severe pain after a major injury. Fever with significant back pain also needs prompt medical attention. Do not wait for a routine therapy visit.

Persistent pain, symptoms traveling down the leg, or a history such as cancer or osteoporosis deserve a discussion with a clinician about the right level of care.

## Questions to ask before choosing care

- What did the examination show, and are other causes or referrals being considered?
- What can I continue doing, and what should I modify?
- What outcome will we measure, and when will we reassess?
- What would make imaging or a physician visit appropriate?
- What are the visit costs and insurance arrangements?
