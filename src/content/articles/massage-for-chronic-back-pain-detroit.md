## Why Chronic Back Pain Outlasts So Many Massages

If you've been dealing with chronic back pain in Detroit, Troy, Warren, Royal Oak, or Rochester, you've probably tried massage—maybe monthly, maybe weekly. It feels good in the moment. And then, within days, the same tightness creeps back.

**This isn't a failure of the massage therapist's skill. It's a limitation of what hands can sustain.** Chronic back pain is rarely caused by one "sore" muscle. It's usually caused by a network of **root muscles—the erector spinae, quadratus lumborum, and psoas among them—pulling your spine out of balance and compressing the structures around it.** These muscles need *sustained, targeted pressure* to release, and that's exactly where manual massage runs into its physical limits.

## Root Muscles Most Responsible for Chronic Back Pain

- **Erector Spinae** — Deep back muscles that chronically brace against pain and poor posture
- **Quadratus Lumborum (QL)** — Often the single tightest, most overlooked contributor to one-sided low back pain
- **Psoas** — A deep hip flexor that, when tight, pulls the lumbar spine into compression
- **Gluteus Medius & Piriformis** — Compensating muscles that tighten when the low back is chronically guarded

Manual massage can reach these muscles, but **holding effective pressure on the psoas or QL for the full duration needed is exhausting even for experienced therapists**—which is exactly why relief so often fades within days.
