## Who treats carpal tunnel near Detroit?

A carpal tunnel specialist may be a hand surgeon, neurologist, rehabilitation physician, or another clinician experienced in evaluating hand numbness. A primary care clinician can also help you choose the next step. A physical therapist may be involved when therapy is appropriate, but ongoing nerve symptoms should not simply be assumed to come from tight muscles.

Carpal tunnel syndrome involves pressure on the median nerve at the wrist. Tingling or numbness often affects the thumb, index, middle, and part of the ring finger and may be noticed at night or while holding a phone or steering wheel. Other problems can mimic these symptoms. See the [AAOS guide to carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.orthoinfo.org/bodypart/hand-wrist/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/) for more information.

## What a useful evaluation includes

Your provider should ask which fingers are affected, whether symptoms wake you, whether you drop objects, and how work or daily tasks affect your hand. The examination may include sensation, thumb strength, wrist movement, and tests that reproduce symptoms. Depending on the findings, a physician may recommend nerve conduction studies or other testing.

Bring a timeline, a list of activities that provoke symptoms, and records of prior splints, injections, therapy, or tests. Ask what findings support carpal tunnel and what other explanations are being considered.

## Treatment choices and questions

Depending on severity, options may include a wrist splint at night, changes to aggravating activities, therapy, an injection, or a surgical opinion. Ask how long to try a proposed option, how sensation and strength will be monitored, and what would make a referral or a different treatment necessary. No single approach fits every case.

## When to seek medical care

Arrange prompt physician assessment for constant numbness, worsening hand weakness, increasing difficulty gripping, or visible muscle loss at the base of the thumb. Sudden one-sided weakness or numbness accompanied by facial droop or speech difficulty is an emergency: call 911.
