## How to avoid lower back pain when playing golf

Golf looks gentle, but the swing is a fast, repeated rotation that asks the hips, trunk, and lower back to work together. Add bending over the ball, practice sessions, walking the course, and lifting a bag, and the lower back can take a lot of cumulative load.

If your back aches during or after a round, you are not alone. This guide explains what a low back flare-up looks like, what golfers commonly notice, how to reduce the chance of repeat flare-ups, and how RPT Clinic can help.

![Golfer setting a tee, a bend that can load the lower back](/health/images/Sports/golfer%20setting%20up%20tee.jpg)

## What is a low back flare-up?

A low back flare-up is a period when pain, tightness, or stiffness in the lower back increases, often after a change in activity or a long stretch in one position. It can be triggered by a round after weeks away from the course, a long range session, or a stiff morning tee time.

A flare-up does not by itself show serious damage. It signals that demand briefly exceeded what your back was ready for. See our overview of [lower back pain](/health/lower-back-pain/).

## Symptoms golfers often notice

- Tightness or a dull ache across the lower back during the round.
- Pain at the top of the backswing or on follow-through.
- Soreness when bending to set up or retrieve the ball.
- Stiffness the next morning or after sitting in the car.
- Tight hips, hamstrings, or glutes alongside back soreness.
- Pain on one side that matches your lead or trail side.

Pain that travels down the leg, numbness, tingling, or weakness needs assessment. See [when to see a doctor for back pain](/health/when-to-see-a-doctor-for-back-pain/).

## Why golf can irritate the lower back

- **Limited hip and upper-back rotation.** If those areas do not rotate well, the lower back may rotate more than it should.
- **Repeated one-sided motion.** Golf loads the same direction thousands of times over a season.
- **Cold start.** Swinging hard without warming up can leave tissues unprepared.
- **Fatigue.** Late-round swings often involve tired muscles.
- **Long sitting before play.** Driving to the course can stiffen the hips. See [back pain from sitting](/health/how-to-relieve-back-pain-from-sitting/).
- **Sudden increases.** Practice binges or a first round of the season can spike load quickly.

## How to avoid lower back pain when playing golf: practical steps

1. **Warm up before you swing.** Walk briefly, then use easy trunk rotations, hip hinges, and gradually larger practice swings.
2. **Improve hip mobility.** Tight hips can push rotation into the lower back.
3. **Strengthen glutes, hips, and trunk.** Strong supporting muscles help share the work of the swing.
4. **Hinge from the hips.** When picking up a ball or bag, bend from the hips rather than rounding the lower back.
5. **Ease back in.** After time away, start with fewer holes or shorter range sessions.
6. **Mind your bag.** Use a cart or a well-fitted strap system if carrying aggravates symptoms.
7. **Stop when pain turns sharp.** Do not swing through worsening pain.
8. **Ask about your swing.** A coach can check whether your setup or sequencing adds avoidable strain.
9. **Reassess if it recurs.** See [why back pain keeps coming back](/health/why-does-my-back-pain-keep-coming-back/).
