## The short answer

**For most non-emergency back pain, the fastest relief comes from stacking simple steps: keep moving gently, ease tight muscles with heat or ice, take pressure off the spine, and stop repeating what triggered it.** If pain lingers, assessment-led care at RPT Clinic in nearby Troy can look at the muscles and movement patterns behind it.

## The top 10

### 1. Keep moving with low-impact activity

Rest briefly if needed, then walk. Easy walking, swimming, or gentle yoga usually helps more than bed rest. Stop if pain turns sharp or spreads.

### 2. Change position every 20 to 30 minutes

Long stretches at a desk or behind the wheel keep the hips and low back in one position. See [how to relieve back pain from sitting](/health/how-to-relieve-back-pain-from-sitting/).

### 3. Use heat for stiff muscles

A heating pad or warm shower for up to 20 minutes can ease tension. Protect your skin.

### 4. Use ice after a sudden strain

A wrapped cold pack for about 20 minutes may dull sharp pain after a twist or lift.

### 5. Try a position of relief

Lie on your back with your calves on a chair, or on your side with a pillow between your knees.

### 6. Do gentle, pain-free stretches

Try child's pose, cat-cow, a single knee to the chest, and light hamstring or glute stretches. See [how to loosen tight hips](/health/how-to-loosen-tight-hips/).

### 7. Build core and hip support once it settles

Bridges, bird-dogs, and beginner yoga or Pilates help share the load. Modify the activity that provokes pain rather than stopping everything.

### 8. Use breathing and relaxation

Slow breathing and progressive muscle relaxation can help muscles that stay braced. See [muscle tightness](/health/muscle-tightness/) and [muscle spasms](/health/muscle-spasms/).

### 9. Consider over-the-counter or topical options

Ask a pharmacist or doctor first. These ease symptoms but do not explain why the back is guarding.

### 10. Get targeted care for the muscles driving the pain

If the pain keeps coming back, read [why back pain keeps coming back](/health/why-does-my-back-pain-keep-coming-back/). An assessment can show whether muscular contributors are involved.

## Michigan triggers that load the back

- **Snow shoveling.** Push snow instead of lifting it, take small loads, and rest every few minutes.
- **Cold-morning stiffness.** Warm up with a few minutes of easy movement before chores.
- **Long drives and commutes.** Adjust the seat, stop when you can, and stretch afterward.
- **Spring and fall yard work.** Raking, bagging leaves, and mulching add repeated bending and twisting.
- **Golf and winter sports.** See our guides for [golf](/health/how-to-avoid-lower-back-pain-when-playing-golf/), [skiing](/health/how-to-avoid-lower-back-pain-when-skiing/), and [snowboarding](/health/how-to-avoid-lower-back-pain-when-snowboarding/).

## Common questions people ask

### Why does my back hurt when I stand up after sitting?

Muscles and joints can stiffen in one position. Standing and moving gently often eases it. If it is sharp or travels down a leg, read [sciatica from sitting](/health/sciatica-from-sitting/).

### Why is my back pain on one side?

One-sided pain can come from muscle tension, joint irritation, or posture. See [left-side back pain](/health/left-side-back-pain/) and [right-side back pain](/health/right-side-back-pain/).

### Is it a pulled muscle or something else?

A strain often feels sore and tight, and it is worse with certain movements. Pain with numbness, weakness, or leg symptoms needs a closer look. See [muscle strain](/health/muscle-strain/).

### Can a pinched nerve cause back pain?

Possibly. Leg pain, tingling, or burning may point to nerve irritation. See [sciatica](/health/sciatica/).

### Should I see a chiropractor, physical therapist, or doctor first?

For red flags, start with a physician. For most other non-emergency cases, an assessment from a physician or physical therapist is a reasonable first step. See [who to see for back pain](/health/back-pain-specialist-near-me-troy-mi/).

## When to get urgent or prompt care

Seek urgent medical care for:

- Progressive weakness in a leg or foot
- Numbness in the groin or saddle area
- New bowel or bladder changes
- Fever or severe pain that rest does not relieve
- Pain after a fall, accident, or significant trauma

See a clinician soon if pain is severe, travels down a leg, or has not improved in a couple of weeks. Read [when to see a doctor for back pain](/health/when-to-see-a-doctor-for-back-pain/).
