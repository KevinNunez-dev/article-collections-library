## The short answer

Groin pain when walking can come from **muscles, the hip joint, a hernia, or the lower back**. The pattern, such as where it is and what triggers it, helps narrow the possibilities, but only a clinician can sort out the cause.

## Common patterns and what they may suggest

| What you notice | Possible contributor |
|---|---|
| Inner thigh and groin ache after sport or a sudden stretch | Adductor (inner thigh) strain |
| Front-of-hip pain on lifting the knee, climbing stairs | Hip flexor irritation |
| Deep, pinching groin pain with hip stiffness | Hip joint problem |
| Bulge or pressure that worsens with standing or straining | Hernia, which needs medical assessment |
| Pain with back stiffness or leg tingling | Lower back or nerve contribution |

This table is for orientation only. It is not a diagnosis.

## Describe it clearly

Before you see anyone, answer:

1. **Where exactly?** Point with one finger.
2. **When did it start?** Gradually or suddenly?
3. **What triggers it?** Walking, stairs, getting out of a car, turning in bed.
4. **What does it feel like?** Sharp, dull, pinching, burning.
5. **Any other symptoms?** Bulge, swelling, numbness, fever, urinary changes.

## What may help while you wait for assessment

- Reduce activities that clearly provoke it
- Walk shorter distances rather than pushing through sharp pain
- Avoid aggressive stretching of a painful area
- Keep a simple log of triggers and what eases it

These are general steps, not medical advice for your situation.

## When to seek urgent care

- A painful bulge in the groin that will not go back in
- Testicular pain or swelling
- Fever with groin pain
- Sudden severe pain, or inability to bear weight
- Numbness in the groin or saddle area, or loss of bladder or bowel control
