## 1. Reduce the Activity That Clearly Aggravates Symptoms

If stairs, deep squats, hills, or running reliably flare pain, temporarily reduce the range, speed, or volume. This is different from avoiding all movement; comfortable activity can help maintain confidence and mobility.

## 2. Build Activity Gradually

A jump in steps, exercise intensity, or training frequency can increase knee load quickly. Use gradual progressions and allow recovery after unfamiliar activity.

## 3. Check the Whole Leg

Hip, thigh, calf, ankle, and foot movement can all affect knee loading. Footwear, prior ankle injury, and reduced hip mobility may be worth considering when knee symptoms recur.

## 4. Avoid Forcing a Painful Range

Pain is useful information. Choose a comfortable range and seek guidance before pushing through sharp pain, catching, or giving-way sensations.

## 5. Get an Individual Assessment

RPT Clinic evaluates movement and muscular tension around the hip, thigh, and lower leg. When appropriate, Robotic Precision Therapy can provide gentle targeted pressure within a broader care plan.
