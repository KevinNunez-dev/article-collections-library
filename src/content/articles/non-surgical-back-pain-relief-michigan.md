## What are non-surgical options for back pain?

Many people with back pain are treated without surgery. A non-surgical plan may include education, activity adjustments, physical therapy, and medical guidance when appropriate. The right combination depends on the cause, your health history, what you want to get back to doing, and how symptoms change.

Non-surgical does not mean ignoring pain or avoiding a physician. It means choosing care based on an assessment and being prepared to revisit the plan. [MedlinePlus's back pain overview](https://medlineplus.gov/backpain.html) describes common causes and treatment options.

## Start with an assessment

Tell the clinician when the pain began, whether it followed an injury, and what happens when you sit, lift, stand, sleep, or walk. Mention pain traveling into a leg, numbness, tingling, weakness, previous care, and relevant medical history.

A clinician may assess back and hip movement, strength, walking, and neurological signs when appropriate. Ask what findings matter, whether a medical evaluation or imaging is needed, and which activities you can safely continue. See our [lower back pain specialist guide](/health/lower-back-pain-specialist-detroit) for questions to ask a provider.

## Build a plan around daily life

A useful plan connects treatment to a goal you can track. For example, you might discuss sitting through a work meeting, walking a familiar route, sleeping more comfortably, or returning to a usual lifting task. A physical therapist can help identify movement and function goals; a physician can advise on medical conditions, medication, or further testing.

Ask how to adjust activities temporarily without assuming that complete bed rest is necessary. Discuss when to reassess and what to do if a treatment worsens symptoms. No treatment or set number of visits guarantees relief. See [non-surgical lower back care in Metro Detroit](/health/nonsurgical-lower-back-pain-treatment-metro-detroit) for additional options.

## When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the saddle or groin area, rapidly worsening leg weakness, or severe pain after major trauma. Fever with significant back pain also needs prompt medical attention. Persistent pain or leg symptoms that interfere with daily life deserve evaluation even when they are not an emergency.
