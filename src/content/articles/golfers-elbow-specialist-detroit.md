## Who treats golfer's elbow near Detroit?

Golfer's elbow usually refers to pain at the inner side of the elbow where forearm tendons attach. It can affect people who do not golf, including those whose work or exercise involves repeated gripping or wrist movement.

A primary care clinician, sports medicine physician, orthopedic or hand specialist, or physical therapist may assess the problem. The best starting point depends on the injury history and whether symptoms suggest a need for medical care. Read more about [golfer's elbow](/health/golfers-elbow) and its symptoms on [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007638.htm).

## What should a provider evaluate?

Describe the precise location of pain and the tasks that provoke it, such as lifting, carrying, gripping tools, or swinging a club. Mention recent changes in activity, previous injuries, and whether you feel tingling in the ring or little fingers.

The examination may include elbow and wrist movement, grip, strength, and the activities that reproduce symptoms. Nerve irritation and other problems can also cause inner elbow symptoms, so ask what findings support the working explanation and whether another assessment is needed.

## When to seek medical care

Seek urgent medical assessment for an obviously deformed elbow after injury or a hot swollen elbow with fever. New substantial weakness, ongoing numbness, or worsening symptoms also deserve prompt evaluation. Do not ignore new neurological symptoms simply because the pain is near a tendon.

## Questions about treatment and activity

- Which work, gripping, or golf activities should I adjust temporarily?
- What exercises are suitable now, and when should they progress?
- Are my finger symptoms consistent with a nerve problem?
- How will we track progress in the activities I want to resume?
- When would testing or referral be appropriate?

There is no guaranteed recovery schedule. A plan should adapt to your response and goals. For outside elbow symptoms, see [tennis elbow](/health/tennis-elbow).
