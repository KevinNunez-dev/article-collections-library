## Why do my back and neck hurt after holiday travel?

Long drives or flights can keep you in one position longer than usual. Lifting luggage, sleeping somewhere unfamiliar, and changes to your normal routine may add to discomfort or aggravate an existing problem. A sore back or neck after travel does not automatically identify the cause.

For persistent, non-emergency discomfort, RPT Clinic in Troy can discuss whether an assessment is appropriate. Robotic Precision Therapy may use the RX2600 Therapeutic Robot to address selected soft tissues when muscular contributors appear suitable after assessment.

## Can a long drive or flight aggravate back pain?

Some people find that a long period of sitting makes back or leg symptoms more noticeable. Seat support and available room to change position can affect comfort, but there is no single perfect sitting posture for everyone. See [how to relieve back pain from sitting](/health/how-to-relieve-back-pain-from-sitting/) and [sciatica from sitting](/health/sciatica-from-sitting/).

If pain travels down a leg or comes with numbness or weakness, tell a clinician. Seek emergency care for the warning signs below.

## Why might your neck feel stiff after a trip?

Looking at a screen for a long time, carrying bags, or sleeping upright or in an unfamiliar bed may affect comfort. Try changing position when safe and supporting your head and neck in a way that feels comfortable. Do not force a painful stretch or assume every headache or arm symptom comes from neck tension. See [neck pain](/health/neck-pain/) and [neck pain from sleeping](/health/how-to-fix-neck-pain-from-sleeping/).

## How can you make holiday travel more comfortable?

1. **Plan breaks.** On a car trip, stop somewhere safe to stand and walk. On a flight, move around when the crew says it is safe.
2. **Adjust your support.** Try a small cushion or rolled-up jacket behind the lower back and adjust your seat to avoid an uncomfortable reach.
3. **Change position.** Shift comfortably and move your feet and ankles while seated when you can.
4. **Pack manageable bags.** Consider rolling luggage, keep loads close when lifting, and ask for help with a heavy bag or overhead bin.
5. **Leave room in the schedule.** A less rushed trip gives you more chances to take breaks and avoid lifting more than you can manage.

The [CDC's travel guidance](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/dvt) discusses movement breaks and blood clot risk during long trips. If you have additional risk factors, ask your clinician about precautions before travel; do not start medication for this purpose without medical advice.

## When should you seek urgent care after travel?

Seek urgent medical assessment for a painful, swollen, warm, or red leg, which can be a sign of a blood clot. **Call 911** for chest pain, sudden difficulty breathing, fainting, or coughing up blood. Do not wait for a therapy visit.

Seek emergency care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or saddle area, or rapidly worsening weakness. Severe pain after a crash or fall also needs urgent medical assessment.
