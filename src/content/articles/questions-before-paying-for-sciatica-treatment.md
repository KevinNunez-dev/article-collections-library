## Before you commit, get the details

Sciatica describes symptoms that can travel from the lower back into the leg; the label alone does not establish the cause. Before paying for a service, ask whether it fits your symptoms and what it includes. You can share these questions with a family member or your existing clinician.

## Questions about assessment and treatment

1. Who will assess me, and what are their qualifications?
2. What will the first visit include? What information or records should I bring?
3. What can this service address, and what can it not do?
4. Do my symptoms or history suggest I should see a physician or specialist first?
5. What alternatives are reasonable, including continuing care I already receive?
6. What goals will we track, when will we review progress, and what if I do not improve?

Bring relevant imaging reports or prior care notes if available, but ask the provider what is needed. Do not stop or change an existing plan without speaking with the clinician managing it.

## Questions about money and scheduling

1. What are the first-visit and follow-up prices and durations?
2. Are there additional charges, and when is payment due?
3. Is a deposit required, does it apply to the bill, and what are the cancellation or refund terms?
4. Is a package optional? What visits does it include, and what are its refund and expiration rules?
5. Does the clinic bill insurance? If not, what documentation can it provide for a claim?
6. How and when will an appointment request be confirmed?

Ask for the answers in writing. An insurer's estimate or a billing code does not guarantee claim payment.

## Warning signs: do not wait for a routine appointment

Seek urgent medical care for new or worsening leg weakness, groin or saddle numbness, new loss of bladder or bowel control, or severe pain after major trauma. The [AAOS sciatica guide](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/sciatica/) explains the condition and care options.
