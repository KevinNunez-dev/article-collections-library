## Why Does My Neck Hurt After Sleeping?

**Neck pain from sleeping** happens when the neck spends hours in a position that stretches or compresses its muscles and joints. You may wake with a **sore neck**, a **painful neck** that is hard to turn, or a **neck ache at night** that lingers into the morning. Most mild cases come from position and muscle tension rather than a serious problem.

Common contributors:

- A pillow that is too high, too flat, or no longer supportive
- Sleeping with the neck rotated, especially on the stomach
- A mattress that lets the shoulders sink or hips sag
- Muscle tension from stress, screens, or recent exercise
- Existing stiffness or prior neck injury

## Best Sleep Positions for Your Neck

### Side Sleeping

Choose a pillow that fills the gap between your ear and the mattress so your head stays level with your spine. A pillow that is too low drops the head toward the bed, and one that is too high tilts it up.

### Back Sleeping

A lower, supportive pillow that cradles the natural curve of the neck works best. Avoid stacking pillows that push the chin toward the chest.

### Stomach Sleeping

This position keeps the neck rotated for hours and is typically hardest on it. If you cannot change, use a very thin pillow and consider placing a pillow under the hips and lower abdomen to reduce strain. Gradually training yourself toward side sleeping may help.

## Pillow Height and Support Checklist

- Head and neck align with the rest of the spine when lying down
- The pillow does not push the chin to the chest or the head backward
- It still feels supportive after months of use
- Shoulders are not squeezed up toward your ears

## Mattress Considerations

A mattress that is very soft can let the shoulders sink and tilt the neck, while an overly firm one can make side sleeping uncomfortable. If your neck hurts mainly in one bed, such as a hotel, that is a hint that setup matters. Adjust gradually; there is no single best mattress for everyone.

## Your 72-Hour Reset (General Guidance)

This is a general approach for mild, non-urgent neck soreness, not an individualized treatment plan.

**Day 1**

- Use gentle, pain-free neck motion every hour or two
- Apply warmth for 10 to 15 minutes if it eases stiffness
- Check your pillow height and sleep position tonight

**Day 2**

- Continue gentle range of motion: slow nods, turns, and ear-to-shoulder tilts within comfort
- Take short walks and keep shoulders relaxed
- Reduce long stretches of screen time or reset your monitor height

**Day 3**

- Add light shoulder blade squeezes and easy posture breaks
- Return to normal activity as comfort allows
- Keep the new sleep setup consistent

If symptoms are not improving after about three days, are getting worse, or include radiating symptoms, seek an assessment.

## Morning Mobility Routine

1. Lie on your back and gently turn your head side to side, within comfort.
2. Slowly tuck your chin and release, 5 to 8 times.
3. Roll your shoulders backward in slow circles.
4. Sit tall and tilt your ear toward your shoulder, holding gently for a few breaths.

Stop any movement that causes sharp, shooting pain or tingling.

## When Physical Therapy Helps

A physical therapy evaluation is worth considering when:

- Morning stiffness keeps recurring
- Pain radiates into the shoulder, arm, or hand
- You have numbness or tingling in the arm or fingers
- Headaches come with neck pain
- Pain does not improve or keeps returning

A therapist assesses neck and upper-back mobility, shoulder blade control, posture, and sleep and work habits.
