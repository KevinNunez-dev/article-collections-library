## Does cold weather make joint and muscle pain worse?

Some people notice more joint aches or muscle stiffness during cold Michigan weather. The connection is not the same for everyone: research has found associations between some weather conditions and pain, but it has not established one simple cause. An ache on a cold day does not by itself mean your joint is damaged.

Changes in routine, activity, and existing health conditions may also affect how you feel. If muscular contributors appear relevant after assessment, RPT Clinic may discuss Robotic Precision Therapy using the RX2600 Therapeutic Robot as one possible part of care.

## Why might your knees, hips, or shoulders feel stiff in winter?

Winter can change how much you walk, exercise, or spend time in one position. You might notice stiffness when you get up after sitting, after clearing snow, or when restarting an activity. These patterns are useful to share with a clinician, but they do not prove that the temperature caused the pain.

For more specific questions, see [knee pain](/health/knee-pain/), [hip pain](/health/hip-pain/), and [shoulder pain](/health/shoulder-pain/).

## What does research say about weather and arthritis pain?

The [Arthritis Foundation's review of weather and pain](https://www.arthritis.org/health-wellness/healthy-living/managing-pain/understanding-pain/weather-arthritis-connection) describes mixed evidence. One study found modest associations with some weather measures, while temperature itself was not significantly associated with pain in that study. Other research has reached different findings.

An association does not establish that weather damaged a joint or caused an individual's symptoms. New swelling, persistent pain, or a change in function deserves assessment rather than being attributed to the forecast.

## What may help with winter aches and stiffness?

- **Dress for the weather.** Layers, gloves, and suitable footwear can help you stay comfortable and reduce exposure.
- **Keep moving within your abilities.** Try comfortable indoor or outdoor movement when conditions are safe; change position after long periods of sitting.
- **Ease into activities.** Take breaks during winter chores and avoid pushing through sharp or worsening pain.
- **Track your pattern.** Note which joints or muscles hurt, how long stiffness lasts, and whether swelling or other symptoms appear.
- **Review existing care.** If you have arthritis or another condition, discuss changes with the clinician managing it. Do not stop prescribed treatment based on weather or this article.

If snow removal is part of your routine, read [lower back pain after shoveling snow](/health/lower-back-pain-after-shoveling-snow/) for precautions, including heart-safety guidance.

## When should you seek urgent care?

Seek urgent medical assessment for a hot, swollen joint with fever, severe pain or inability to bear weight after a fall, or an obviously deformed joint. A painful swollen calf also needs urgent evaluation. Call 911 for chest pain or difficulty breathing. Seek a clinician's advice for pain or stiffness that persists, worsens, or limits daily activities.
