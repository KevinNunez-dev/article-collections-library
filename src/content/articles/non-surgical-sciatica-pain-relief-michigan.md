## Can sciatica be treated without surgery?

Many people with sciatica begin with non-surgical care. Sciatica describes symptoms such as pain, tingling, or numbness that can travel from the lower back or buttock into the leg. It is a symptom pattern rather than a single diagnosis, so care should be based on an assessment rather than the label alone.

Depending on the cause and examination, a plan may involve physical therapy, changes to daily activity, medical treatment, or a combination. Surgery is not automatic, but a medical or surgical evaluation should not be delayed when serious symptoms are present. The [AAOS sciatica guide](https://www.orthoinfo.org/bodypart/back/sciatica/) explains common symptoms and treatment options.

## What should a sciatica assessment include?

Describe where the pain begins and the farthest point it reaches in the leg. Mention numbness, tingling, weakness, and what happens when you sit, drive, walk, bend, or sleep. Bring prior imaging reports and a list of treatment you have tried if available.

A clinician may assess walking, back and hip movement, leg strength, sensation, and reflexes when appropriate. Ask whether the pattern suggests nerve involvement, whether other causes are being considered, and what symptoms would change the plan. Our [sciatica specialist guide](/health/sciatica-specialist-detroit) explains how to compare providers.

## Non-surgical options and practical goals

Depending on the findings, options may include tolerable activity, physical therapy, medication advice from a qualified clinician, and injections for selected cases. A provider can help adjust activities that repeatedly worsen symptoms without assuming you must stop moving entirely.

Choose a meaningful goal, such as a comfortable commute, walking for a specific time, or sleeping with fewer interruptions. Ask when to review leg symptoms, strength, sensation, and function. If symptoms travel farther, new numbness appears, or walking changes, contact the clinician managing your care.

## When to seek urgent medical care

Seek urgent medical care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the saddle or groin area, rapidly worsening leg weakness, or severe pain after major trauma. Fever with significant back pain also needs prompt medical attention. Do not wait for a routine therapy appointment.

Persistent or worsening symptoms may require a physician evaluation, imaging, or a specialist opinion. Ask your clinician what follow-up is appropriate rather than continuing an ineffective plan indefinitely.
