## Poor Posture Relief: Restore Optimal Alignment

Poor posture doesn't result from lack of willpower—it results from **muscle imbalance**. Modern life (desk work, phones, driving) creates tight chest muscles, weak upper back stabilizers, and tight hip flexors that pull your body out of alignment.

**The consequence: Forward head posture increases stress on your neck by 10 lbs per inch of displacement, leading to chronic neck pain, headaches, and accelerated spine degeneration.**

At **RPT Clinic**, we use the **RX2600 Therapeutic Robot** to release the tight muscles pulling you forward, allowing your body to naturally return to proper alignment. Most patients notice postural improvement within 6-10 sessions.

## Understanding Poor Posture

Good posture isn't about standing tall—it's about balanced muscle tension maintaining neutral spine alignment. When muscles become imbalanced:

- **Tight chest muscles** pull shoulders forward
- **Weak upper back** cannot hold proper position
- **Tight hip flexors** tilt pelvis forward, creating lower back curve
- **Weak deep core** cannot support the spine
- Result: Forward head posture, rounded shoulders, swayback

This misalignment creates:
- Increased stress on neck and lower back
- Nerve compression
- Accelerated spine degeneration
- Chronic pain development
- Reduced movement efficiency

## Common Poor Posture Effects

## The Root Cause: Muscle Imbalance

**Poor posture stems from specific muscle imbalances**:

**Tight Pectoralis Minor & Major** pull shoulders forward and compress chest

**Weak Rhomboid & Lower Trapezius** cannot retract shoulders

**Tight Hip Flexors (Psoas)** tilt pelvis forward, creating lower back curve

**Weak Glutes** cannot stabilize the pelvis

**Tight Chest** combined with weak back creates forward head posture

**Sedentary Lifestyle** compounds all these imbalances

## Why Posture Coaching Alone Fails

Simply "thinking about" good posture doesn't work because tight muscles keep pulling you forward. The moment you're distracted, tight muscles win. **You need to address the root: muscle imbalance.**
