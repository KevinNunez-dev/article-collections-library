## Before your insurance call

Coverage for lower back pain or sciatica therapy depends on your plan, the clinician, and the specific service. Have your member information, the provider's name and identifying details, and a description of the proposed service. If available, ask the provider for the codes and documentation it expects to supply. Do not assume a code confirms coverage.

## Ask about network and eligibility

- Is this specific provider in my plan's network for this service?
- If the provider does not bill insurance, can I submit a claim myself?
- Does my plan have out-of-network benefits for this service and provider?
- Is a physician referral, prescription, or prior authorization required?
- Are there plan-specific exclusions or medical-necessity requirements?

## Ask what you may owe

- What is my remaining deductible, and does it apply to this service?
- Is there a copay or coinsurance? How is it calculated?
- What allowed amount or reimbursement limit might apply?
- Are visits limited, and are they shared with other therapies?
- Could the provider charge more than the plan recognizes, and would I owe the difference?

An insurer's estimate is not a final claim decision. Ask for any applicable plan information in writing. [HealthCare.gov's total-cost guide](https://www.healthcare.gov/choose-a-plan/your-total-costs/) explains common insurance cost terms.

## If you may submit your own claim

Ask what an invoice must contain, whether proof of payment is needed, how to submit the claim, and the filing deadline. Ask how you will receive the decision and what to do if it is denied. Record the date, representative's name, and reference number. Keep copies of your documents in a secure place.
