## Who should you see for knee pain near Detroit?

A knee pain specialist could be a primary care clinician, sports medicine physician, orthopedic specialist, or physical therapist. A new injury, marked swelling, locking, or difficulty bearing weight may require a medical evaluation. For other persistent limitations, a clinician can help decide whether physical therapy or another type of care is appropriate.

Knee pain is not one diagnosis. Pain in the front during stairs, pain along the inner or outer joint, and pain after a twist can have different explanations. The location and history help guide an examination, but do not confirm a cause on their own. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/kneeinjuriesanddisorders.html) describes common knee injuries and disorders.

## What should a knee evaluation include?

Explain when the pain started, whether there was a twist or fall, and what happens when you walk, squat, climb stairs, or get up from a chair. Mention swelling, catching, locking, giving way, and any previous knee surgery.

Depending on your symptoms, the clinician may assess walking, knee motion, strength, balance, and the hip and ankle. Ask what findings support the working explanation, whether imaging is needed, and which activities you can safely continue.

## When to seek urgent care

Seek urgent medical assessment for a hot, swollen knee with fever; a visibly deformed knee; or inability to bear weight after an injury. A knee that locks and cannot straighten or gives way repeatedly also deserves timely medical assessment. Ask a clinician how quickly you should be seen.

## How to compare treatment plans

Ask how your provider will measure progress: for example, walking time, ability to climb stairs, or returning to a specific activity. Discuss how to adjust activities without unnecessarily stopping everything, when to reassess, and what happens if pain or swelling increases. Treatment choices depend on the diagnosis; not every knee problem needs an injection or surgery.
