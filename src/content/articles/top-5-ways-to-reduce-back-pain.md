## 1. Keep Movement Frequent and Comfortable

Long periods in one position can make back symptoms feel worse. Break up sitting, standing, and driving with brief, comfortable changes in position. The goal is not to push through pain but to avoid letting one posture dominate the day.

## 2. Adjust the Load Before Adding More Exercise

A sudden increase in lifting, running, bending, or household tasks can exceed current tolerance. Reduce or modify the activity that consistently provokes symptoms, then build back gradually as comfort improves.

## 3. Use Simple Workstation and Sleep Changes

Set screens near eye level, keep commonly used items close, and vary your working posture. Sleep comfort can also be affected by how the hips and back are supported, so small adjustments may be worth testing.

## 4. Support the Hips and Upper Back

The back works with the hips, legs, and upper body. Movement limits or muscle tension elsewhere can shift load to the low back. An assessment should consider the full movement pattern.

## 5. Get an Assessment When Pain Persists

RPT Clinic evaluates muscular tension and movement restrictions that may contribute to non-emergency back pain. Robotic Precision Therapy can provide gentle, targeted pressure as part of an individualized plan.

Seek urgent care for progressive weakness, bowel or bladder changes, numbness in the saddle area, fever, or significant trauma. [Contact RPT Clinic](https://rptclinic.com/contact/) for persistent back symptoms.
