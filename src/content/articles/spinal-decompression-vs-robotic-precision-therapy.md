## What is the difference between spinal decompression and RPT?

**Nonsurgical spinal decompression generally refers to traction: a pulling force applied to the spine.** Robotic Precision Therapy (RPT) at RPT Clinic uses the RX2600 Therapeutic Robot to apply pressure to selected soft tissues under clinician direction. The approaches are different, and neither guarantees relief.

The word decompression can also mean **surgery** to relieve pressure on spinal nerves or the spinal cord. Ask which procedure a provider means; nonsurgical traction is not the same as an operation.

## What happens during each approach?

| Question | Nonsurgical spinal decompression | Robotic Precision Therapy |
|---|---|---|
| What is applied? | A pulling force, often using a table or equipment | Pressure from the RX2600 to selected soft tissues |
| What should you ask? | How traction is delivered, why it is proposed, and its risks | What is assessed, where pressure is applied, and how it can be adjusted |
| What is not established here? | That traction will repair a disc or work for every person | That RPT repairs a disc, releases a nerve, or works better than traction |

Practices and devices vary. Ask each provider exactly what a session includes and whether it is appropriate for your history.

## What does the evidence say about traction?

The UK [NICE guideline for low back pain and sciatica](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59/chapter/recommendations) recommends **not offering traction** for managing low back pain with or without sciatica. It also discusses activity, exercise, and other care options. That recommendation applies to traction for these conditions; it does not establish how the RX2600 compares with traction or prove the effectiveness of RPT.

Ask for independent evidence specific to the proposed treatment and your condition. Claims that either approach reliably repairs discs or avoids surgery should not substitute for medical advice.

## Can RPT treat a herniated disc or compressed nerve?

RPT may address appropriate muscular contributors after assessment. The RX2600 does **not** repair or reposition a disc, decompress the spine, or release a compressed nerve. Disc-related pain or weakness may need medical evaluation and, in some cases, specialist care. See [herniated disc pain](/health/herniated-disc/) and [sciatica](/health/sciatica/).

## What should you ask before choosing care?

1. What is the likely cause of my symptoms, and do I need medical evaluation?
2. What is the goal of the proposed treatment, and what evidence supports it?
3. What are the risks, alternatives, and total cost?
4. How will we measure progress, and when will we reconsider the plan?
5. Which changes mean I should stop and seek urgent care?

## When should you seek urgent medical care?

Seek emergency care for new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or saddle area, or rapidly worsening leg weakness. Severe pain after major trauma or fever with severe back pain needs urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for a routine therapy appointment.
